                    ETHICAL DECISION MAKING AND CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

A guiding checklist for councillors, staff and community committees

Ethical decision making

   •   Is the decision or conduct legal?
   •   Is it consistent with Government policy, Council’s objectives and Code of Conduct?
   •   What will the outcome be for you, your colleagues, the Council, anyone else?
   •   Does it raise a conflict of interest?
   •   Do you stand to gain personally at public expense?
   •   Can the decision be justified in terms of public interest?
   •   Would it withstand public scrutiny?

Conflict of interest

A conflict of interest is a clash between private interest and public duty. There are two types of conflict:

   •   Pecuniary – an interest that you have in a matter because of a reasonable likelihood or
       expectation of appreciable financial gain or loss to yourself or another person or entity defined in
       part 4 of the Lismore City Council Code of Conduct with whom you are associated.
   •   Non-pecuniary – a private or personal interest that you have that does not amount to a pecuniary
       interest as defined in the Lismore City Council Code of Conduct. These commonly arise out of
       family or personal relationships, or out of involvement in sporting, social, religious or other
       cultural groups and associations, and may include an interest of a financial nature.

The test for conflict of interest

   •   Is it likely I could be influenced by personal interest in carrying out my public duty?
   •   Would a fair and reasonable person believe I could be so influenced?
   •   Conflict of interest is closely tied to the layperson's definition of "corruption" – using public office
       for private gain.
   •   Important to consider public perceptions of whether you have a conflict of interest.

Identifying problems

   •   Do I have private interests affected by a matter I am officially involved in?
   •   Is my official role one of influence or perceived influence over the matter?
   •   Do my private interests’ conflict with my official role?

Disclosure and participation in meetings

   •   A Councillor or a member of a Council Committee who has a pecuniary interest in any matter
       with which the Council is concerned and who is present at a meeting of the Council or Committee
       at which the matter is being considered must disclose the nature of the interest to the meeting as
       soon as practicable.
   •   The Councillor or member must not be present at, or in sight of, the meeting of the Council or
       Committee:

            a.   at any time during which the matter is being considered or discussed by the Council or
                 Committee, or
            b.   at any time during which the Council or Committee is voting on any question in relation
                 to the matter.

No Knowledge - a person does not breach this clause if the person did not know and could not
reasonably be expected to have known that the matter under consideration at the meeting was a matter
in which he or she had a pecuniary interest.
Non-pecuniary Interests - Must be disclosed in meetings.

There are a broad range of options available for managing conflicts & the option chosen will depend on
an assessment of the circumstances of the matter, the nature of the interest and the significance of the
issue being dealt with. Non-pecuniary conflicts of interests must be dealt with in at least one of the
following ways:

   •   It may be appropriate that no action be taken where the potential for conflict is minimal. However,
       Councillors should consider providing an explanation of why they consider a conflict does not
       exist.
   •   Limit involvement if practical (eg. Participate in discussion but not in decision making or vice
       versa). Care needs to be taken when exercising this option.
   •   Remove the source of the conflict (eg. Relinquishing or divesting the personal interest that
       creates the conflict)
   •   Have no involvement by absenting yourself from and not taking part in any debate or voting on
       the issue as of the provisions in the Code of Conduct (particularly if you have a significant non-
       pecuniary interest)

Whilst seeking advice is generally useful, the ultimate decision rests with the person concerned.

Acting Director Infrastructure Services
As indicated in the proposed Notice of Motion, the Lismore Lake precinct is subject to a number of planning
documents that guide the activities that can be undertaken in that area. All of these documents are quite
old and out of date given a number of circumstances that have changed over time. For example:

   •   The lake, being man-made, is not water tight and “leaks”.
   •   Council’s ability to manage water levels in the lake for bird habitat and/or other activities has been
       severely affected by changes to water licensing processes to extract water from the Wilsons River
       – Council no longer holds a license for this purpose.
   •   Water quality in the lake is questionable and cannot be relied upon as suitable for activities such
       as swimming and boating etc.
   •   The closure of the Lake Pool some time ago has changed the use of the surrounding areas.
   •   An EOI process conducted in October 2021 for community groups to work with Council to revitalise
       the Lake Pool area did not yield any responses.
   •   Increasing activity and future plans for the Lismore Airport will potentially influence decisions
       around what occurs at the lake and surrounding areas as bird activity in an aviation precinct
       represents a hazard to aircraft.

As such the existing Plans of Management (POMs) need to be reviewed and updated to reflect current
activities, challenges and aspirations at the site and reflect proposed future uses, which are likely to be
very different from historical use.

From that perspective any activities at the site, other than vegetation management and regeneration,
should be held in abeyance until a master planning exercise for the entire area can be completed and
POMs updated to reflect Council and the community’s future vision for the precinct.

In this regard staff have been looking for opportunities for funding of a master planning process for some
time, without success. Consideration of including funding for a master planning exercise and review of the
POM in the upcoming Delivery Program, Operational Plan and budget processes is recommended.

There will be a cost for this work in the form of staff time to prepare the proposed license, oversee the
activities of the group and pursue funding opportunities.

General Manager

Manager Commercial Services (Resource Recovery)
Currently waste vouchers are available via online application and redemption at either the Lismore
Recycling and Resource Recovery Facility or Nimbin Waste Transfer Facility. This system is completely
automated, and only requires manual intervention from staff if an issue arises with a specific application
being processed. Vouchers are made available electronically and on request without staff resources
required, and to minimise the cost and volume of resource consumption (paper) from printing hard copy
voucher that may not be redeemed. The system was developed to minimise administration required by
Council staff and the associated costs.

FY21.22 operational plan includes a total 5,000 vouchers at an approximate value of $61 per voucher,
and total budget allowance of $305,400. The cost of these vouchers is covered by the Waste
Minimisation Levy, which is generated from some 18,873 rateable properties in the Lismore LGA.

Under the current voucher arrangements 1,250 vouchers were available for Q1 FY21.22. 477 vouchers
were redeemed over this period. This is approx. 38% redemption rate of the vouchers available. Unused
vouchers remain available for use for the remainder of the FY, and Council has since advertised that the
unused vouchers are available in addition to the usual monthly rate of availability. There is no evidence
to suggest that supply within the community is not being met by the number of vouchers being made
available online each month.

An alternative system for allocation and distribution of waste vouchers would be possible to create, and
an estimated cost and timeframe for the necessary software programming can be obtained as desired.
Any expected possible improvement / cost benefit from the current online request system would need to
be investigated and reported back to Council to ensure transparency.
Alternate arrangements for voucher distribution with variations to the number and value of vouchers are
possible, but need to be funded and provided for in Council’s adopted budget. The difference in cost for
providing those vouchers (increased or reduced) would be distributed across the Waste Minimisation
rate base. The Waste Minimisation rate base is all rateable properties of the LGA other than those
paying the Nimbin Transfer Station annual charge (approx. 330 properties).

Classification of Public Land
The LG Act defines public land as any land vested in or under Council control and is managed under the
LG Act based on its classification. Classification of public land may occur either by an amendment to the
Lismore Local Environment Plan (LEP) or by resolution of Council when first acquired. Councils are
encouraged to classify land by Council resolution where suitable.

The purpose of classification is to identify clearly that land which is made available for use by the public
(Community) and that land which need not (Operational). How public land is classified determines the
ease or difficulty a Council can have in dealing with the public land. It also provides for transparency in
Council’s strategic asset management.

Section 31 of the LG Act stipulates any land acquired by Council that is not classified before it is acquired
or within three months after it is acquired, will default to Community land. Any future reclassification would
then require an amendment to the LEP. As such, this report requests Council to classify Lot 91 now as
Operational, at a time before it is acquired. Following the attached and web link to Registrar General's
Guidelines for drainage reserves, the Operational land classification is suggested as appropriate because
it is consistent with the use of the land for drainage purposes.

Background
There are numerous current panel contracts and projects for which the General Manager had previously
been provided delegation to manage, with authorization to approve purchases from these contracts and
for these projects without any upper limit of expenditure.

These contracts have followed an open tendering process either through Lismore City Council or through
a prescribed entity. They work as a pre-selection of suppliers with which we enter into a deed of standing
offer.

This allows Council to then engage a contractor directly or to conduct a selective tendering process with
all or limited panel members for specific works and therefore to reduce the procurement process
timeframe, which can be critical when works impact directly the community and/or the funding grant is
dependent on a set completion date.

A panel arrangement is a way to procure goods or services regularly acquired by entities. In a panel
arrangement, suppliers have been appointed via an open tender process to supply goods or services for
a set period of time under agreed terms and conditions, including agreed pricing.

Local Government Procurement (LGP) has been prescribed under s55 of the Local Government Act
1993 (NSW), allowing councils to utilise supply arrangements coordinated by LGP without the need to go
to tender in their own right. LGP therefore has the same status as NSW Procurement in having this
legislative requirement.

What is the authority of Local Government Procurement to tender on behalf of councils?
LGP has been ‘prescribed’ by the NSW State Parliament to carry out group tenders on behalf of NSW
local government. ‘Prescribed’ means LGP is named in the Local Government (General) Regulation 2005
(NSW) and as such a council does not need to go to tender if that council buys from a contract already set
up by LGP. Councils can therefore procure goods or services from LGP contracts for values greater than
$250,000 (inclusive of GST), without the need to tender themselves.

Obviously, they can also procure for amounts less than $250,000 without restriction from LGP contracts.

Other Local Government prescribed entities are:
   • NSW Procurement (ex-ProcurePoint, now BuyNSW), and
   • Procurement Australasia
   • NSW Public Works Advisory

Current situation
The purpose of the delegation to General Manager to authorise works from pre-selected contractors is to
ensure projects can progress in the shortest possible timeframe, as the community expects, and to comply
with grant timelines.

One specific example is the current CBD footpath project on Keen Street. Selective tenders from a pre-
existing civil works panel (T20/3) have been called out and will close on 4 February 2022. With a delegation
in place, the contract could be awarded and successful contractors moving to establish on site by 18
February 2022 (two weeks after the closing date). If the decision to award the tender is required to go to
Council, the earliest the works can commence is 22 March 2022 (two weeks after the March Council
meeting), which amounts to a delay of 4.5 weeks. Furthermore, there has been a concerted community
engagement effort to align the start date with the expectations of the adjacent business owners, who
strongly expressed a preference to commence the works as soon as possible in the new year, which is
typically the quietest time for businesses - any delays may have adverse impacts.

             The writer has requested additional signage to be installed.
             Corndale Road is a primary collector road under Council’s road hierarchy. It is a two-way
             dual lanes undivided, sealed road, approximately 7m wide and provides connectivity between
             several rural villages to the Bruxner Highway HW62. The area is a rural setting with Corndale

             sign ahead; a One Lane Bridge and Give Way sign on the southern approach to the bridge.
             The line marking requires some repainting, but essentially complies with AS1742.2. There is

             In regard to TfNSW Technical Reference and its application for this site, the greater sight
