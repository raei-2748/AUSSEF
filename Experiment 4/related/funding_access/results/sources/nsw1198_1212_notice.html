17 December, 2025

The NSW Small Business Commission extends our deepest condolences to all those affected by the devastating terror attack at Bondi Beach last Sunday. We are working closely with other government agencies and organisations to provide immediate support to small businesses in Bondi and surrounds who are grappling with the immediate impacts, together with businesses further afield who are also impacted in various ways.

Please remember, we are open throughout the summer period and here to support businesses impacted by the Bondi attack, and all NSW small businesses in the coming weeks. 

A support package has been made available providing financial assistance, expanded victim support services and other targeted help. This includes a grant up to $25,000 for impacted small businesses in the immediate vicinity to the attack together with consideration for exceptional circumstances where businesses may be outside the immediate vicinity and impacted by the attack.  

Businesses can register for support from today by attending the Community Hub in North Bondi or contacting Service NSW on 13 77 88 to organise a meeting with a Business Concierge. For further information on the financial supports available please visit: https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/community-support-bondi

Looking to the end of the year, our December edition brings together practical end-of-year advice, Shop Local initiatives and key reforms small businesses should be aware of before the new year begins.  

In the lead up to Christmas and the New Year, many small businesses are in their busiest time. We know that for some of you, it won’t be until January or when school returns that you have the chance to come to grips with the regulatory changes coming in 2026 and what they mean for your business. Whenever that time is, if you need help, please get in touch with us. 

FEATURE ARTICLES 

Support Local Businesses 

Supporting local businesses delivers clear economic, social and environmental benefits for communities across NSW - sustaining local jobs, strengthening regional economies, supporting vibrant town centres and reducing environmental impacts. These benefits are on display this festive season through Small Business Friendly Christmas initiatives, including Shop Local campaigns, community gift card programs and Christmas markets across metropolitan and regional NSW. Together, these initiatives help local businesses connect with customers, boost holiday trade and reinforce the value of keeping spending local. Article: Support local this holiday season 

Important Update: Superannuation Changes for 2026 

We also provide an important update on the upcoming closure of the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House ahead of the introduction of Payday Super. With super contributions required within seven business days of payday from July 2026, employers will need to transition to new payment systems and ensure accurate fund details and strong record-keeping processes are in place. Article: Closure of the Small Business Superannuation Clearing House Ahead of Payday Super | NSW Small Business Commissioner 

Your End-of-Year Compliance and Planning Guide 

Our End-of-Year Checklist for Small Businesses offers a quick guide to staying compliant and prepared over the holiday period, covering public holiday penalty rates, staffing and leave planning, festive marketing opportunities, cash-flow management and regulatory housekeeping. The Commission’s mediation and customer service teams will remain available throughout Christmas and New Year to support businesses needing assistance.  Article: End-of-Year Checklist for Small Businesses – 2025 | NSW Small Business Commissioner 

Key Regulatory Changes Coming in 2026 

We also outline the major new laws and reforms coming in 2026. These include new anti-money laundering obligations for professional services, quarterly reporting under the portable long service leave scheme, mandatory FOGO waste collection for food businesses, updated environmental requirements, strengthened building and strata compliance standards, enhanced early childhood safety rules, changes to community services funding programs and Australia’s new national merger approval regime. Article: What NSW Small Businesses Need to Know: New Laws, New Rules, New Opportunities in 2026 | NSW Small Business Commissioner 

Recovery Grants Extended to February 2026 

The Category C Recovery Grant for Small Businesses and Non-Profit Organisations has been extended to 2 February 2026. This extension, approved by the Minister’s Office and the National Emergency Management Agency (NEMA) applies to applications under AGRN 1198 (Tropical Cyclone Alfred Severe Weather) and AGRN 1212 (NSW East Coast Severe Weather). Eligible organisations now have extra time to submit claims for financial assistance. Full details are available via Service NSW: https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/small-business-recovery-grant-agrn-1198-1212 

ATO Warning  

The ATO has warned it is cracking down on barter credit tax schemes, flagging them as a priority compliance risk in Taxpayer Alert TA 2025/3. These schemes involve inflated tax deductions for donated barter credits and may be treated as illegal tax avoidance. Businesses, advisers and charities are urged to review their arrangements, seek independent advice, and make voluntary disclosures where needed to reduce potential penalties. Article: ATO to crack down on barter credit tax scheme 

Expression of Interest to join the New Small Business Consultative Group 

The Australian Government is also inviting small business to participate in a new Small Business Consultative Group (SBCG). The Group, established by the Minister for Small Business, will advise on national small business policy. The Terms of Reference are available at treasury.gov.au/small-business and EOIs close on 16 January 2026. This is an opportunity for NSW small businesses and sector leaders to contribute directly to future policy development. Article: Small Business Consultative Group to give small businesses a direct voice 

Momentum Survey 

The November Momentum Survey is now available. It shows small business confidence declined between October and November 2025, decreasing by four percentage points to 25 per cent and more than reversing last month’s modest two percentage point increase. This fall in confidence coincides with inflation data coming in higher than expected, as well as the RBA’s decision to leave the cash rate unchanged at its 4 November meeting. 

Despite confidence levels falling in November, the Momentum Index, which measures underlying business trends, as opposed to sentiment (which may be influenced by broader narratives as well as businesses’ own direct experiences), rose by 6.6 points to 120.7, a record high, and remained in expansionary territory for a fourth consecutive month. This was primarily driven by an increase in businesses planning to purchase or rent additional property, plant and/or equipment, and a significant rise in businesses planning to hire new staff, potentially in anticipation of increased trade leading up to the Christmas and New Year period.
