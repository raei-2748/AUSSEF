# Decision-unit audit

The CSV unit is location × AGRN; the policy decision is generally event × state × measure with a geographic scope. Beneficiary or provider eligibility and disbursement occur at another level. These units must not be conflated.

| Layer | Observed? | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Location–AGRN flag | Yes | Recorded category indicator in one snapshot |
| Named measure and category | Partly: 42 C components in 10 events | Program classification, not independent approval count |
| Formal named geographic scope | Partly | Availability within listed geography, not universal resident/council receipt |
| Full considered geographic risk set | No | Needed to distinguish eligible negatives from areas never considered |
| Applicant / beneficiary decision | No | Needed for access or refusal outcome |
| Payment | No | Needed for actual receipt outcome |

Thirty C-positive AGRNs and eight mixed events are descriptive counts. There are zero certified eligible contrasts. The count of independent approval decisions is unknown, not zero. Generic regional mental-health provision, council recovery officers, tourism support and business grants are not exchangeable outcomes.

AGRN 1046 is the decisive scope check: three Category C programs name 5, 3 and 6 eligible councils. Cloncurry and Mornington have raw C=0 but are on the tourism list. Preserve this discrepancy. Jasper includes an explicit Wujal Wujal council-targeted C measure inside an all-C event. South Australia's 1209 is an unincorporated-area program; Bondi 1240 is a terrorist event. Neither creates an ordinary-council natural-disaster comparison.

Do not count multiple LGAs as multiple independent policy decisions. Do not presume distinct AGRNs are independent storms. Require decision identifiers and shared-package / physical-event-family links before any later evaluation.
