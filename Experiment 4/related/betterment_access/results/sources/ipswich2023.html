COUNCILLOR PAUL TULLY

In accordance with section 150EQ of the Local Government Act 2009, Councillor Paul Tully informed the meeting that he has a declarable conflict of interest in Item 10 of the Governance and Transparency Committee Report titled Repeal of Previous Council Decision and Proposed New Trustee Lease over 135 Brisbane Terrace, Goodna to Goodna and Districts Rugby League Football Club Inc.

 

The nature of the interest is that Councillor Tully was a former president of the Goodna Rugby League Club and is currently an ordinary member of the club.

 

Councillor Paul Tully invited the other councillors to determine if he can continue to participate in the decision process.

there is no personal or financial benefit to the councillor and therefore a reasonable person would trust that the final decision is made in the public interest.

 

The eligible councillors present at the meeting decided that Councillor Paul Tully may participate in the meeting in relation to the matter, including by voting on the matter.

Executive Summary

This report is for the month of April and summarises organisational performance in the areas of workplace health and safety, progress in the delivery of Council’s Corporate and Operational Plans, our people and culture, financial management, capital program delivery and significant risks and legal matters.

 

The organisation continues to remain focused on our ongoing service delivery while ensuring the recovery and rehabilitation of Council’s assets within the city that were impacted by the February and May 2022 significant weather events. Annual Plan, Capital Program and Operational Budget 2023-2024

 

Significant work has been undertaken by the organisation to prepare the Annual Plan, Capital Program and Operational Budget for the 2023-2024 financial year.  This has included the identification and recommendation of strategic, capital and operational priorities in line with Council’s Corporate Plan iFuture for the Mayor, Deputy Mayor and Councillors to consider at the Special Council Meeting on 22 June 2023. Purpose of Report/Background

This report outlines the financial results for Ipswich City Council at 30 April 2023. The actuals results have been compared to the amended budget which was approved by Council in April.

The total net result (including capital revenue) for Ipswich City Council as at 30 April 2023 is $89.2 million compared to the year to date (YTD) budget of $78.6 million.

Council’s YTD operating deficit (excluding capital revenue) is approximately $3.6 million compared to the YTD budget deficit of $2.4 million.

Overall, capital expenditure including the Nicholas Street Redevelopment YTD but excluding the flood buy-back and flood recovery work is $600k over budget. Asset donations as at
30 April 2023 are $15.5 million below the YTD budget.

 

Revenue

Operating revenue is now $2.6 million over budget YTD, after $15.6 million of additional operating revenue was included in the budget through the April 2023 budget amendment.

Rates and utilities revenue is ahead of budget by $950k due to growth in residential rates, a lower portion of rate payers taking advantage of on time payment discounts than expected and other rates adjustments.

Waste disposal fees are tracking ahead of budget by $957k due to a greater volume of services being provided to commercial business.

Grant revenue is ahead of budget by $18.8 million, primarily due to $20 million being recognised for the Voluntary Home Buy Back scheme YTD. OTHER RELEVANT INFORMATION:

 

Built Form and Amenity Impacts

 

The Large Lot Residential Code and the Community Use Code specify that non-residential uses are to maintain “a scale and appearance in keeping with the residential amenity and character of the locality with adequate buffering or screening to nearby residential uses” and that “the character, scale, height and intensity of development…” is to be –

 

“…commensurate with the intent of the zone or Sub Area in which the development is proposed…; compatible with the physical characteristics of the site and its surrounds; and compatible with the desired character of the local area.”

 

Furthermore, development is to - 

 

“…provide reasonable buffers between incompatible uses…” and “not impose unreasonable adverse impacts on and …[be] compatible with the existing and likely future amenity of the nearby area.”

 

Throughout the assessment process, the assessment manager repeatedly highlighted that the design of the built form does not comply with the outcomes sought for the zone in terms of the proposed site coverage, bulk of the building, boundary setbacks and provision of landscaped buffers, and raised concerns regarding amenity impacts on adjoining residential properties. Whilst the applicant revised the design of the proposal in response to Council’s information request (see ‘Change to the Application’ below) which went some way towards addressing Council’s concerns, the final proposal plans submitted by the applicant maintained a bulk and scale that is still not considered to be consistent with the intent of the zone and, in fact, increased site coverage (i.e., from 24% to 33%), reduced the boundary setbacks and landscape buffers.

 

Change to the application

 

On 14 November 2022, the applicant provided a response to Council’s Further Advice correspondence, which included a change to the original development proposal. The change incorporated modifications to the built form, car parking and landscaping design, which addressed some of Council’s concerns; however, the change also included an increase to the site coverage and number of child care places and staff numbers which were originally proposed (i.e. 11 additional child care places, increased from 76 places, and 2 additional staff, increased from 11 staff). Upon assessment of the proposed changes, the assessment manager advised the applicant that the change was not considered to be a ‘minor change’ to the application in accordance with the Planning Act 2016, and the changes were not solely in response to matters raised in a submission, information request or further advice. Furthermore, the assessment manager was not satisfied that the change would not be likely to attract a submission objecting to items comprising the change.

 

Thus, in accordance with section 27.1 of the Development Assessment Rules, the development assessment process stopped on 14 November 2022 and recommenced at the beginning of the Confirmation Period. The assessment manager subsequently issued a new information request indicating concerns with the amended proposal in terms of compliance with the Large Lot Residential and Community Use Codes and including technical items which the applicant had not adequately responded to previously (i.e., relating to stormwater quantity and quality, and waste collection/traffic).

 

The applicant provided a partial response to the information request which indicated that they would work with Council on responding to technical matters to the extent that they cannot be conditioned, if in-principle support was given by Council for the revised design outlined above. The applicant then commenced the second public notification period.

 

Despite assessment manager advice to the applicant recommending that a full response be provided, including further revision of the proposal plans (Council directed the applicant to an example of an outcome negotiated during an appeal for a similar development proposal), no further response was provided upon completion of public notification. After multiple attempts by the assessment manager to contact the applicant, the applicant instructed Council on 17 April 2023 to decide the application with the information provided. Whilst some of the following matters outlined below could be resolved through the imposition of reasonable and relevant conditions, the nature and number of unresolved impacts associated with the proposed development are not insignificant and the interaction of the numerous conditions to be applied may have unintended consequences. Furthermore, it is considered that the consequences of an approval outweighing the community benefits offered by the proposed development.

 

In accordance with Council’s ‘Framework for Development Applications and Related Activities Policy’, owing to the application receiving more than 20 properly made submissions objecting to the proposed development, the application is required to be decided by Council (rather than under delegation).

 

Parking

 

The applicant indicated that the parking provision for the proposed development complies with the child care centre rate specified under the Parking Code of the Ipswich Planning Scheme, based on 87 childcare places and 13 full time equivalent (FTE) staff. However, in accordance with the minimum centre-based educator to child ratio requirements under the National Quality Framework (NQF) for child care centres, the proposed development requires a minimum of 12 FTE educators, which has not accounted for all FTE ancillary/support staff that would reasonably be required to service a child care centre (such as centre director, administration, cook, lunch relief staff, etc.). Consequently, the proposed parking is not considered to comply with the specific outcomes of the Parking Code.  It is also noted that a No Standing sign restricts on-street parking in Old Logan Road across the frontage of the land and owing to the access and traffic movements required for the site, parking in Woodlands Avenue would also have to be restricted should the proposed development be approved.  Therefore, this lack of available on-street parking exacerbates the need to provide sufficient parking on the site.

 

Community Need

 

The applicant provided an Economic Need and Impact Assessment for the development to demonstrate that proposed non-residential use will fulfill a local community need. Whilst it is acknowledged that there are other child care centres within the vicinity of the subject site, the submitted report and subsequent addendum demonstrated, to the satisfaction of the assessment manager, that there is currently sufficient community need within the service area for the number of long day care places proposed.

 

Acoustic Management

 

The applicant provided a Noise Impact Assessment report to consider the potential noise amenity impacts of the proposed development on nearby sensitive receivers. The report concluded that the proposed child care centre development can comply with the relevant noise criteria during the proposed operating hours of 6am to 6pm Monday to Friday, with occasional refuse collection during the evening period (6pm to 10pm) and the inclusion of the following acoustic barriers which achieve a minimum surface density of 12.5kg/m2:

 

·    2m high acoustic barrier to the west of the ground floor outdoor play area (with the top of the barrier a minimum 2m above the finished level of the outdoor play area);

·    2.2m high acoustic barrier to the north of the ground floor outdoor play area (with the top of the barrier a minimum 2.2m above the finished level of the outdoor play area); and

·    1.8m high solid balustrade/acoustic screen above the finished floor level along the northern, western and southern perimeters of the first-floor outdoor play area.

 

Notwithstanding the above recommendations, Council advised the applicant that waste collection outside operating hours is not supported in this location, due to noise amenity impacts on adjoining residential development. However, no further response was provided to Council’s concerns.

 

In addition, whilst the proposed location of the acoustic barriers (internal to the site rather than on the boundary) is intended to avoid unacceptable visual amenity impacts on adjoining neighbours resulting from a combined retaining wall and acoustic barrier height on the boundary of up to 5m, the current proposal results in an equally unacceptable outcome wherein the proposed landscaping will be obscured between two (2) solid fences. Council raised concerns about the visibility and accessibility (for maintenance) of the proposed landscaping; the applicant suggested in their response that a landscape maintenance strategy could be conditioned for compliance but provided no further details of what the maintenance strategy might entail, nor how the visibility of the landscaping would be improved.

 

Stormwater Quantity and Quality

 

The subject site slopes down from the south-east corner towards the north-west, with levels ranging from approximately 58.37m to 54.23m AHD. The applicant provided an Engineering Report and Stormwater Management Plan which proposed to capture and mitigate stormwater runoff from most of the development site via a 30m3 underground detention tank, prior to discharge to kerb and channel in Woodlands Avenue.

 

In relation to stormwater quality, the Stormwater Management Plan did not detail how the development achieves the water quality objectives outlined in Table 2.3.1 of Planning Scheme Policy 3 ‘General Works’. The applicant’s initial response indicated that treatment is not required; however, Council advised the applicant that stormwater quality treatment is triggered in accordance with Council’s Implementation Guideline 24 ‘Stormwater Management’, owing to the proposal including over 600m2 of uncovered impervious car park area. No further response was provided by the applicant in relation to this matter.

 

Traffic

 

The applicant provided a Traffic Impact Assessment which included, inter alia, an assessment of the impacts of the proposed development on the Woodlands Avenue/Old Logan Road/Cochrane Street intersection and the Woodlands Avenue/site access intersection. The analysis confirmed that both intersections would operate acceptably within various traffic volume scenarios up to a 10-year design horizon. Notwithstanding, Council issued a further information request on 30 May 2022 requesting that the applicant consider a right turn auxiliary lane from Woodlands Avenue into the proposed development, to provide safe refuge for vehicles turning right into the centre and to allow for uninterrupted flow of traffic in Woodlands Avenue, owing to safety concerns associated with the site’s proximity to the intersection.

 

The applicant provided a Traffic Technical Note in response, which included an assessment of the additional traffic impacts resulting from the increased number of proposed child care places. The response indicated that the additional traffic generation would still be within acceptable limits utilising the existing intersection configuration. Notably, the report concluded that westbound traffic on Woodlands Avenue can pass a right-turning vehicle (rather than queuing behind), owing to the carriageway width of 9.5m. Based on the analysis provided, it is agreed that an auxiliary turning lane or other mitigation works would not be required for the proposed development.

 

Waste Management

 

The applicant proposed that waste collection for the development will be undertaken by a 10.3m long rear-loading refuse collection vehicle which will access the site in forward gear, then reverse at the end of the carpark within the site and temporarily stand to the south of the pedestrian path/tandem parking, where bins will be manually wheeled from the bin store. It was originally proposed that servicing would be undertaken between the hours of 6pm to 10pm.

 

The proposal is not considered to be acceptable owing to noise amenity impacts associated with servicing during evening/night-time hours, and safety impacts associated with passenger vehicle and pedestrian conflicts without provision of a management plan.

 

Council requested further information to demonstrate how waste collection servicing will be appropriately managed (ensuring the service vehicle can enter and exit the site in forward gear) and that all waste collection will occur during operating hours; however, the applicant’s response indicated that refuse collection arrangements will be finalised at a later stage. As such, the noise amenity and associated safety impacts associated with waste management have not been adequately resolved.

 

Earthworks and Landscaping

 

The applicant has proposed to undertake extensive fill to provide a level development site.  As outlined in the description of the proposal above, the development plans include 1.5m wide and 1.7m wide battered landscaping areas, proposed to be situated between 1.8m high Colorbond fences on the northern and western property boundaries (atop 1m high retaining walls) and a timber paling acoustic fence within the site (atop additional retaining walls varying in height from 500mm to 1.8m). The proposed landscape buffers are not considered to be adequate in width to provide the desired amenity outcomes for the adjoining residential uses. Furthermore, owing to its proposed situation between boundary and acoustic fences, the landscaping will be largely out of sight of both the subject development and adjoining sites, which raises concerns about both its utility and maintenance.

 

Sewerage

 

In addition to the above matters, Council provided advice to the applicant which highlighted that the existing sewer line to the northern property boundary may not be sufficient to service the development, and that its upgrade may be warranted. The applicant was advised to liaise with Urban Utilities and provide formal correspondence confirming any requirements prior to Council deciding the application; however, no response was received in relation to this matter.
