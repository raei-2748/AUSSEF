April 2023 AUTHOR: Chief Executive Officer DATE: 16 May 2023 Executive Summary This report is for the month of
April and summarises organisational performance in the areas of workplace
health and safety, progress in the delivery of Council’s Corporate and
Operational Plans, our people and culture, financial management, capital
program delivery and significant risks and legal matters. The organisation continues to
remain focused on our ongoing service delivery while ensuring the recovery and
rehabilitation of Council’s assets within the city that were impacted by
the February and May 2022 significant weather events. Recovery and
rehabilitation works will continue throughout 2023 and 2024. The report for this period
highlights current significant matters and progress on key performance
indicators. RECOMMENDATION That the Chief Executive
Officer Organisational Performance Report for the month of April 2023 be
received and the contents noted. BACKGROUND 1.      Current
Significant Matters Annual Plan, Capital Program and Operational Budget
2023-2024 Significant work has been undertaken by the organisation to
prepare the Annual Plan, Capital Program and Operational Budget for the
2023-2024 financial year.  This has included the identification and
recommendation of strategic, capital and operational priorities in line with
Council’s Corporate Plan iFuture for the Mayor, Deputy Mayor and
Councillors to consider at the Special Council Meeting on 22 June 2023. The
recommended capital program will include the work that needs to be done in the
areas of asset rehabilitation and reconstruction as part of the flood recovery
program. Planes, Trains and Automobiles City Event 29 April to 1
May Working with a range of event
partners and sponsors, the Council team coordinated and delivered a hugely
successful Planes, Trains and Automobiles event for the community and visitors
to the Ipswich Local Government Area.  Following the official opening of
the event on Saturday 29 April, thousands of community members and visitors
flocked to the series of events over the three (3) day festival including at
venues The Workshop Rail Museum, Nicholas Street Precinct and Evan Marginson
Park, Goodna. 2.      Workplace
Health and Safety There was very good safety performance across council, with
the injury and incident rates well below the same time last year.  The
physical and psycho-social safety and wellbeing of the workforce continues to
be the CEO and the organisation’s first and foremost priority. The Lost Time Injury Frequency Rate (LTIFR) is 2.34
which is slightly higher than the month of March (1.85). This equates to 2.34
lost time injuries every six (6) months. Within the LGAQ scheme of 62 Councils,
the state average LTIFR is 8.23, noting the ICC rate is significantly less than
the state average. Lost time severity rate is 34.01* which is lower than
the month of March (48.2*). One employee will be off work for at least one (1) year,
with another returning to work in early March.  State average is 36.97
days lost per LTI. 3.      Update on

separate report on the Council agenda. The April 2023 year to date
result now includes a comparison to the amended budget which was approved by

capital revenue) is less than budget with a net deficit of $3.6 million

adjusted for in the budget amendment, and only minor adjustments were made to
the AISD capital program. Capital Delivery Program
2022-2023 The Capital Delivery Program continues to progress well
against the full-year budget allocation. Some delays due to material and
contractor availability are being experienced, however these are effects that
are being felt across the broader construction industry. Some cost deferrals will be seen in projects such as the
tri-Council Material Recycling Facility payment, boardwalks at White Rock and
for the large construction projects at Springfield-Greenbank Arterial and
Redbank Plains Road Stage 3. 5.      Risk

Government Regulation 2012 , be considered and noted by Council. RELATED PARTIES Not applicable. ifuture Theme A Trusted and Leading Organisation Purpose of Report/Background This report outlines the financial
results for Ipswich City Council at 30 April 2023. The actuals results have
been compared to the amended budget which was approved by Council in April. The total net result (including
capital revenue) for Ipswich City Council as at 30 April 2023 is $89.2
million compared to the year to date (YTD) budget of $78.6 million. Council’s YTD operating
deficit (excluding capital revenue) is approximately $3.6 million compared to
the YTD budget deficit of $2.4 million. Overall, capital expenditure
including the Nicholas Street Redevelopment YTD but excluding the flood
buy-back and flood recovery work is $600k over budget. Asset donations as at 30 April 2023 are $15.5 million below the YTD budget. Revenue Operating
revenue is now $2.6 million over budget YTD, after $15.6 million of additional
operating revenue was included in the budget through the April 2023 budget
amendment. Rates and utilities revenue is
ahead of budget by $950k due to growth in residential rates, a lower portion of
rate payers taking advantage of on time payment discounts than expected and other
rates adjustments. Waste disposal fees are tracking
ahead of budget by $957k due to a greater volume of services being provided to
commercial business. Grant revenue is ahead of budget
by $18.8 million, primarily due to $20 million being recognised for the
Voluntary Home Buy Back scheme YTD. This timing variance will reverse by
30 June 2023, when the budget phasing is recognised.  This is
partially offset by the Financial Assistance Grants being under budget due to a
greater percentage than expected being received in the previous financial year. Other revenue is tracking ahead of
budget for the year by $1.7 million which is due to additional tax equivalent
revenue from Urban Utilities and profit from the sale of fleet assets. Donated asset and cash contributions
revenue is $7.8 million below the YTD budget which is driven primarily by asset
donations being $15.5 million lower than budget expectations, and partially
offset by cash contributions being $7.8 million above budget. Expenses Overall operating expenses are
approximately $3.8 million over YTD budget. Employee expenses (including
labour contracts) are over budget by $827k which is mainly due to the use of
contract labour, overtime, and allowances. Overtime mainly relates to Resource
Recovery (associated with truck downtime) and staffing requirements for Works
and Field Services. Materials
and services (excluding labour contracts) are over budget $1.5 million which is
due to higher fuel prices, flood recovery expenditure, legal appeals and an unallocated
efficiency target of $2.5 million.  These overspends are partially offset
by lower-than-expected cleaning, security, power, and water costs for the
Nicholas Street Precinct. Depreciation is over budget YTD
and includes increased depreciation expenses as a result of revaluation
increments recorded for the Roads, Bridges and Footpaths, Buildings and
Structures and Drainage asset classes. These over budget amounts are partially
offset by lower than expected capitalised projects and the impact of the
change in accounting treatment of software as a service at 30 June 2022. Flood Recovery The total operating and capital
expenditure on flood recovery since the flood events is $40.8 million, of

Recommendation - Property Projects Australia Childcare Centre at Camira AUTHOR: Planner (Development) DATE: 24 April 2023 Executive Summary This is a report concerning an
application seeking approval for a Material Change of Use - Community Use
(Child Care Centre) at 1 Woodlands Avenue, Camira. The proposed development has been
assessed with regard to the applicable assessment benchmarks.  The
proposed Community Use (Child Care Centre) cannot be supported in accordance
with section 5 and section 60 of the Planning Act 2016 , as the proposal
does not advance the purpose of the Act and the development conflicts with the
applicable codes of the Planning Scheme with no sufficient grounds to justify
the decision despite the conflict. Recommendation That Council resolve to refuse
development application no. 1714/2022/MCU for the reasons contained in
Attachment 1 of this report . RELATED PARTIES The related parties to this application are: · Applicant –

Notification Consultant – Development Signs ifuture Theme Vibrant and Growing Purpose of Report/Background SITE ADDRESS: 1 Woodlands Avenue, CAMIRA  QLD  4300 APPLICATION TYPE: Material Change of Use PROPOSAL: Material Change of Use - Community Use (Child Care Centre) ZONE: LLR3 – Large Lot Residential OVERLAYS: Not applicable APPLICANT: Property Projects Australia Pty Ltd OWNER: Mr R J Nilsen and Mrs C L Nilsen EXISTING OR PROPOSED TRADING NAMES: Oak Tree Academy APPLICATION NO: 1714/2022/MCU AREA: 1,865m 2 REFERRAL AGENCIES: Not applicable EXISTING USE: Single residential PREVIOUS RELATED APPROVALS: Not applicable DATE RECEIVED: 21 February 2022 DECISION PERIOD START DATE: 10 March 2023 EXPECTED DETERMINATION DATE: 27 April 2023 SITE LOCATION: Figure 1 – Site location SITE DETAILS AND SURROUNDING LAND USES: The subject site is a 1,865m 2 corner lot in
Camira, which is currently improved by a lowset single residential dwelling and
ancillary buildings and structures, enclosed by privacy fencing along both
street frontages. The site is accessed via a single driveway crossover from
Woodlands Avenue and is adjoined by single residential development to the north
and west and bound by Old Logan Road to the east. The property is mapped within the Large Lot Residential Zone
(sub area LLR3). The land slopes from the highest point in the south-eastern
corner towards the north-west, with a fall of approximately 4m across the site.
The Planning Scheme mapping does not identify any development constraints over
the site. The subject site currently benefits from connection to water
and sewerage networks. PROPOSAL: The applicant is seeking approval for a Material Change of
Use application for a Community Use (Child Care Centre), which is intended to
provide for up to 87 child care places and 12 educators, as outlined in Table
1, below. Table 1 ROOM PLACES AGE STAFF RATIO EDUCATOR NO. Room 1 12 0-2 1:4 3 Room 2 22 4-5 1:11 2 Room 3 33 3-4 1:11 3 Room 4 20 2-3 1:5 4 TOTALS 87 12 The proposed development includes a two-storey building
located centrally on the subject site and orientated towards Woodlands Avenue.
The building design includes 616m 2 of site coverage and a total of
639m 2 of gross floor area (GFA) across the two (2) floors
(specifically, 490m 2 GFA on the ground floor and 149m 2 GFA on the first floor). The building is proposed to be constructed of a
combination of fibre cement weatherboard panelling and brick on the
façades, with sheet metal roofing. The existing residential buildings
and structures are intended to be demolished. The ground floor is comprised of the entry foyer and reception,
three (3) activity rooms, two (2) sleep rooms, a kitchen and preparation rooms,
a laundry and drying yard, toilets, meeting room and staff/planning room
facilities.  The first floor of the building is comprised of a fourth
activity room, plus toilets and preparation room. In addition, the development
includes a total of 630m 2 of outdoor play areas, located on the
ground level (475m 2 ) and on the upper level (155m 2 ). Vehicular access for the development is proposed from
Woodlands Avenue via a 6.3m wide driveway crossover. The applicant proposes 24
car parking spaces to service the development, including 2 spaces in tandem
arrangement (to be allocated to staff) and 1 PWD space. Pedestrian access is
also proposed from Old Logan Road (where the main building entry is located). In addition to the outdoor play areas, the development plans
include battered landscaping areas, 1.5m wide and 1.7m wide, on the northern
and western boundaries, respectively. The landscaping is proposed to be
situated between 1.8m high Colorbond fences on the northern and western
property boundaries (atop 1m high retaining walls) and a timber paling acoustic
fence within the site (atop additional retaining walls varying in height from
500mm to 1.8m). The child care centre is proposed to operate between 6am to
6pm Monday to Friday. OTHER RELEVANT INFORMATION: Built Form and Amenity Impacts The Large Lot Residential Code and the Community Use Code
specify that non-residential uses are to maintain “ a scale and
appearance in keeping with the residential amenity and character of the
locality with adequate buffering or screening to nearby residential uses” and that “ the character, scale, height and intensity of
development…” is to be – “…commensurate
with the intent of the zone or Sub Area in which the development is
proposed…; compatible with the physical characteristics of the site and
its surrounds; and compatible with the desired character of the local area. ” Furthermore, development is to - “…provide
reasonable buffers between incompatible uses…” and “not impose unreasonable adverse impacts on and …[be] compatible
with the existing and likely future amenity of the nearby area.” Throughout the assessment process, the assessment manager
repeatedly highlighted that the design of the built form does not comply with
the outcomes sought for the zone in terms of the proposed site coverage, bulk
of the building, boundary setbacks and provision of landscaped buffers, and
raised concerns regarding amenity impacts on adjoining residential properties.
Whilst the applicant revised the design of the proposal in response to
Council’s information request (see ‘Change to the
Application’ below) which went some way towards addressing
Council’s concerns, the final proposal plans submitted by the applicant
maintained a bulk and scale that is still not considered to be consistent with
the intent of the zone and, in fact, increased site coverage (i.e., from 24% to
33%), reduced the boundary setbacks and landscape buffers. Change to the application On 14 November 2022, the applicant provided a response to
Council’s Further Advice correspondence, which included a change to the
original development proposal. The change incorporated modifications to the
built form, car parking and landscaping design, which addressed some of
Council’s concerns; however, the change also included an increase to the
site coverage and number of child care places and staff numbers which were
originally proposed (i.e. 11 additional child care places, increased from 76
places, and 2 additional staff, increased from 11 staff). Upon assessment of
the proposed changes, the assessment manager advised the applicant that the change
was not considered to be a ‘minor change’ to the application in
accordance with the Planning Act 2016 , and the changes were not solely
in response to matters raised in a submission, information request or further
advice. Furthermore, the assessment manager was not satisfied that the change
would not be likely to attract a submission objecting to items comprising the
change. Thus, in accordance with section 27.1 of the Development
Assessment Rules, the development assessment process stopped on 14 November
2022 and recommenced at the beginning of the Confirmation Period. The
assessment manager subsequently issued a new information request indicating
concerns with the amended proposal in terms of compliance with the Large Lot
Residential and Community Use Codes and including technical items which the
applicant had not adequately responded to previously (i.e., relating to
stormwater quantity and quality, and waste collection/traffic). The applicant provided a partial response to the information
request which indicated that they would work with Council on responding to
technical matters to the extent that they cannot be conditioned, if
in-principle support was given by Council for the revised design outlined
above. The applicant then commenced the second public notification period. Despite assessment manager advice to the applicant
recommending that a full response be provided, including further revision of
the proposal plans (Council directed the applicant to an example of an outcome
negotiated during an appeal for a similar development proposal), no further
response was provided upon completion of public notification. After multiple
attempts by the assessment manager to contact the applicant, the applicant
instructed Council on 17 April 2023 to decide the application with the
information provided. Whilst some of the following matters outlined below could
be resolved through the imposition of reasonable and relevant conditions, the
nature and number of unresolved impacts associated with the proposed development
are not insignificant and the interaction of the numerous conditions to be
applied may have unintended consequences. Furthermore, it is considered that
the consequences of an approval outweighing the community benefits offered by
the proposed development. In accordance with Council’s ‘Framework for
Development Applications and Related Activities Policy’ , owing to the
application receiving more than 20 properly made submissions objecting to the
proposed development, the application is required to be decided by Council
(rather than under delegation). Parking The applicant indicated that the parking provision for the
proposed development complies with the child care centre rate specified under
the Parking Code of the Ipswich Planning Scheme , based on 87 childcare
places and 13 full time equivalent (FTE) staff. However, in accordance with the minimum
centre-based educator
to child ratio requirements under the National Quality Framework (NQF) for child care centres, the proposed development requires a minimum of 12 FTE
educators, which has not accounted for all FTE ancillary/support staff that
would reasonably be required to service a child care centre (such as centre
director, administration, cook, lunch relief staff, etc.). Consequently, the
proposed parking is not considered to comply with the specific outcomes of the
Parking Code.  It is also noted that a No Standing sign restricts
on-street parking in Old Logan Road across the frontage of the land and owing
to the access and traffic movements required for the site, parking in Woodlands
Avenue would also have to be restricted should the proposed development be
approved.  Therefore, this lack of available on-street parking exacerbates
the need to provide sufficient parking on the site. Community Need The applicant provided an Economic Need and Impact
Assessment for the development to demonstrate that proposed non-residential use
will fulfill a local community need. Whilst it is acknowledged that there are
other child care centres within the vicinity of the subject site, the submitted
report and subsequent addendum demonstrated, to the satisfaction of the
assessment manager, that there is currently sufficient community need within
the service area for the number of long day care places proposed. Acoustic Management The applicant provided a Noise Impact Assessment report to
consider the potential noise amenity impacts of the proposed development on
nearby sensitive receivers. The report concluded that the proposed child care
centre development can comply with the relevant noise criteria during the
proposed operating hours of 6am to 6pm Monday to Friday, with occasional refuse
collection during the evening period (6pm to 10pm) and the inclusion of the
following acoustic barriers which achieve a minimum surface density of 12.5kg/m 2 : · 2m high acoustic
barrier to the west of the ground floor outdoor play area (with the top of the
barrier a minimum 2m above the finished level of the outdoor play area); · 2.2m high acoustic
barrier to the north of the ground floor outdoor play area (with the top of the
barrier a minimum 2.2m above the finished level of the outdoor play area); and · 1.8m high solid
balustrade/acoustic screen above the finished floor level along the northern,
western and southern perimeters of the first-floor outdoor play area. Notwithstanding the above recommendations, Council advised
the applicant that waste collection outside operating hours is not supported in
this location, due to noise amenity impacts on adjoining residential
development. However, no further response was provided to Council’s concerns. In addition, whilst the proposed location of the acoustic
barriers (internal to the site rather than on the boundary) is intended to
avoid unacceptable visual amenity impacts on adjoining neighbours resulting
from a combined retaining wall and acoustic barrier height on the boundary of
up to 5m, the current proposal results in an equally unacceptable outcome
wherein the proposed landscaping will be obscured between two (2) solid fences.
Council raised concerns about the visibility and accessibility (for maintenance)
of the proposed landscaping; the applicant suggested in their response that a
landscape maintenance strategy could be conditioned for compliance but provided
no further details of what the maintenance strategy might entail, nor how the
visibility of the landscaping would be improved. Stormwater Quantity and Quality The subject site slopes down from the south-east corner
towards the north-west, with levels ranging from approximately 58.37m to 54.23m
AHD. The applicant provided an Engineering Report and Stormwater Management
Plan which proposed to capture and mitigate stormwater runoff from most of the
development site via a 30m 3 underground detention tank, prior to
discharge to kerb and channel in Woodlands Avenue. In relation to stormwater quality, the Stormwater Management
Plan did not detail how the development achieves the water quality objectives
outlined in Table 2.3.1 of Planning Scheme Policy 3 ‘General
Works’. The applicant’s initial response indicated that treatment
is not required; however, Council advised the applicant that stormwater quality
treatment is triggered in accordance with Council’s Implementation
Guideline 24 ‘Stormwater Management’, owing to the proposal
including over 600m 2 of uncovered impervious car park area. No
further response was provided by the applicant in relation to this matter. Traffic The applicant provided a Traffic Impact Assessment which
included, inter alia , an assessment of the impacts of the proposed
development on the Woodlands Avenue/Old Logan Road/Cochrane Street intersection
and the Woodlands Avenue/site access intersection. The analysis confirmed that
both intersections would operate acceptably within various traffic volume
scenarios up to a 10-year design horizon. Notwithstanding, Council issued a
further information request on 30 May 2022 requesting that the applicant
consider a right turn auxiliary lane from Woodlands Avenue into the proposed
development, to provide safe refuge for vehicles turning right into the centre
and to allow for uninterrupted flow of traffic in Woodlands Avenue, owing to
safety concerns associated with the site’s proximity to the intersection. The applicant provided a Traffic Technical Note in response,
which included an assessment of the additional traffic impacts resulting from
the increased number of proposed child care places. The response indicated that
the additional traffic generation would still be within acceptable limits
utilising the existing intersection configuration. Notably, the report
concluded that westbound traffic on Woodlands Avenue can pass a right-turning
vehicle (rather than queuing behind), owing to the carriageway width of 9.5m.
Based on the analysis provided, it is agreed that an auxiliary turning lane or
other mitigation works would not be required for the proposed development. Waste Management The applicant proposed that waste collection for the
development will be undertaken by a 10.3m long rear-loading refuse collection
vehicle which will access the site in forward gear, then reverse at the end of
the carpark within the site and temporarily stand to the south of the
pedestrian path/tandem parking, where bins will be manually wheeled from the
bin store. It was originally proposed that servicing would be undertaken
between the hours of 6pm to 10pm. The proposal is not considered to be acceptable owing to
noise amenity impacts associated with servicing during evening/night-time
hours, and safety impacts associated with passenger vehicle and pedestrian
conflicts without provision of a management plan. Council requested further information to demonstrate how
waste collection servicing will be appropriately managed (ensuring the service
vehicle can enter and exit the site in forward gear) and that all waste
collection will occur during operating hours; however, the applicant’s
response indicated that refuse collection arrangements will be finalised at a
later stage. As such, the noise amenity and associated safety impacts
associated with waste management have not been adequately resolved. Earthworks and Landscaping The applicant has proposed to undertake extensive fill to
provide a level development site.  As outlined in the description of the
proposal above, the development plans include 1.5m wide and 1.7m wide battered
landscaping areas, proposed to be situated between 1.8m high Colorbond fences
on the northern and western property boundaries (atop 1m high retaining walls)
and a timber paling acoustic fence within the site (atop additional retaining
walls varying in height from 500mm to 1.8m). The proposed landscape buffers are
not considered to be adequate in width to provide the desired amenity outcomes
for the adjoining residential uses. Furthermore, owing to its proposed
situation between boundary and acoustic fences, the landscaping will be largely
out of sight of both the subject development and adjoining sites, which raises
concerns about both its utility and maintenance. Sewerage In addition to the above matters, Council provided advice to
the applicant which highlighted that the existing sewer line to the northern
property boundary may not be sufficient to service the development, and that
its upgrade may be warranted. The applicant was advised to liaise with Urban
Utilities and provide formal correspondence confirming any requirements prior
to Council deciding the application; however, no response was received in
relation to this matter. ASSESSMENT BENCHMARKS: The application is Impact Assessable and has been assessed
against the assessment benchmarks set out by the categorising instruments in
accordance with section 45(5)(a)(i) of the Planning Act 2016. The relevant assessment benchmarks which have been applied
for the purposes of this assessment are as follows: Categorising

pursuant to the Planning Act 2016 .  The applicant initially
undertook public notification from 5 May to 27 May 2022 for a period of 15
business days. On 14 November 2022, the applicant provided a response to
Council’s Further Advice correspondence, which included a change to the
application incorporating modifications to the built form, car parking and
landscaping design, and also included an increase to the number of child care
places and staff numbers proposed (i.e., 11 additional child care places and 2
additional staff). Upon assessment of the proposed changes, the assessment
manager advised the applicant that the change was not considered to be a
‘minor change’ to the application in accordance with the Planning
Act 2016 , and the changes were not solely in response to matters raised in
a submission, information request or further advice. Furthermore, the
assessment manager was not satisfied that the change would not be likely to
attract a submission objecting to items comprising the change. Thus, in accordance with section 27.1 of the Development
Assessment Rules, the development assessment process stopped on 14 November
2022 and recommenced at the beginning of the Confirmation Period. The applicant
was also required to start the Public Notification period again. The second
public notification period was undertaken by the applicant from 2 February to
23 February 2023. Council received forty-two (42) properly made submissions
over the two (2) public notification periods; twenty-six (26) were received
during the initial notification period, and three (3) new submissions were
received during the second period (the remainder were resubmissions in response
to the changed development). A summary of the matters raised in the submissions include: Matters raised How matters were dealt with in reaching a decision Scale, Character and Appearance of Proposed Development · The scale,
  character and appearance of the proposed development (including retaining
  walls and fencing) does not conform with the character of the neighbourhood,
  nor with the Large Lot Residential Zone and Community Use Codes. · The proposed
  site coverage is inconsistent with other properties in the LLR Zone. · The proposal
  presents substantial bulk and dominance to the road frontages and adjacent
  properties, which is inconsistent with the intent of the zone/locality. · The proposed
  development will have a detrimental impact on the character of the street and
  neighbourhood. · The development
  is not incorporated with existing/proposed community nodes; the development
  is better suited to the Springfield area or a nearby neighbourhood centre. · Rather than
  decreasing the scale of the development or number of children of the proposed
  centre in response to Council’s information request, the applicant
  increased the scale, child numbers and staff numbers. The submissions received are common material for the
  application and have been considered as part of the application
  assessment.  The assessment manager agrees with many of
  submitters’ concerns, particularly regarding the proposed scale, bulk
  and appearance of the proposed development and amenity impacts, and lack of
  resolution of some of the technical matters. Whilst some matters could be resolved through the
  imposition of reasonable and relevant conditions, the remaining potential
  impacts and risks associated with the proposed development are not
  insignificant, with the consequences of an approval outweighing the community
  benefits offered by the proposed development. The assessment manager considers that the current proposal
  cannot be appropriately conditioned to address all matters raised and that
  there are insufficient grounds to justify a decision to approve the
  development. The application has been refused for the reasons outlined in
  Item 1 – Reasons for the Decision. Amenity Impacts · The proposed
  development will result in unacceptable acoustic impacts on adjoining
  residences and down Woodlands Avenue from the outdoor play areas, waste
  collection and vehicle movements. · The proposed
  development will result in unacceptable visual amenity impacts (owing to the
  site’s topography, the proposed boundary setbacks, limited landscape
  buffers and upper level outside play area) which are not considered
  acceptable in a Large Lot Residential area. · The proposed
  development will have detrimental impacts on the privacy of residents, owing
  to overlooking into adjacent properties. Community Need for Childcare Centre · There is no
  community need for the CCC due to other childcare centres within the
  vicinity. · The community
  need for the centre is not sufficiently justified by the Economic Need and
  Impact Assessment. Traffic/Road Safety Impacts · The development
  will result in increased traffic congestion at an existing busy and dangerous
  intersection (i.e. Woodlands Avenue/Old Logan Road) and on the surrounding
  road network. · The CCC will
  result in road safety impacts, including those associated with potential
  queuing across the intersection. · The subject site
  has been impacted by traffic accidents in which vehicles penetrated the fence
  along Old Logan Road. · Pedestrian
  safety in Woodlands Avenue will be impacted due to increased traffic
  accessing the development, and because there is no existing footpath. Parking · Insufficient
  parking spaces are proposed for the development, which will result in safety
  and amenity impacts from overflow on-street parking. Waste Collection · There are safety
  issues associated with the access of waste vehicles to the proposed CCC and
  manoeuvrability within the site. · The applicant is
  proposing a rear-lift waste collection vehicle, which is less common than
  front-lift. · Limiting waste
  servicing to particular hours may be impractical, with the operator likely to
  default to out of hours servicing to avoid conflicts with traffic and pedestrians. · The applicant
  has not responded to Council’s information request, nor community
  concerns, regarding waste collection. Stormwater Impacts · The volume of
  water to be discharged from the development will cause safety impacts and
  increase flooding in the low area at the bottom of the hill in Woodlands
  Avenue. · The applicant
  has not responded to Council’s information request, nor community
  concerns, regarding stormwater quantity and quality. Demolition/Construction Impacts · The demolition
  and construction works will disrupt nearby residents and commuters on Old
  Logan Road. Water/Sewer Network · Concern that the
  proposed development will impact the adequacy of the existing reticulated
  water supply to supply the site and maintain service to the rest of the
  community. · Concern that the
  sewer discharge pipe is inadequate to service the development. General Concerns (not planning grounds) · The internal
  design of the development does not comply with ChildSafe policy requirements
  relating to provision of toilet and staff facilities. · The applicant
  has made no attempt to create a ‘green development’ through the
  provision of solar power, rainwater storage, etc. · Staff will not
  be permitted to smoke around children/on the property, so smokers will leave
  the property and sit in cars parked on the street or on the footpath, leaving
  rubbish behind. Conclusion The proposed Material Change of
Use - Community Use (Child Care Centre) at 1 Woodlands Avenue, Camira cannot be
supported in accordance with section 5 and section 60 of Planning Act 2016 ,
as the development conflicts with the applicable codes of the Planning Scheme
with no sufficient grounds to justify the decision despite the conflict. 
Specifically, the proposal does not comply with the overall and specific
outcomes of the Urban Areas Code (Part 4)—particularly the specific
outcomes in section 4.3.3 and the Large Lot Residential Zone (division 4), the
Community Use Code (Part 12, division 12) and the Parking Code (Part 12,
division 9) of Ipswich Planning Scheme 2006 . HUMAN RIGHTS IMPLICATIONS HUMAN RIGHTS IMPACTS OTHER DECISION Human Rights Checklist (a)  What

Shop Leases Act 1994 and Property Law Act 1974 . The right of the lessee to
exercise the option is subject to certain conditions, including giving notice
to the lessor that they wish to exercise the option during the timeframe
specified in the lease (for example, between six (6) and three (3) months prior
to the expiry of the lease) and not being in breach of the lease when they give
notice that they wish to exercise the option. The effect of an option being
exercised is that the lessee continues to lease the premises on the same terms
and conditions for the length of the option (for example, an additional five (5)
years). The essential lease terms are as

  enter into a new lease with the Lessor for a 5 year’ term for the
  provision of a public library. (b) What
  human rights are affected? • 
  Recognition and equality before the law (section 15) • 
  Protection from torture and cruel, inhuman or
  degrading treatment (section 17) • 
  Freedom of movement (section 19) • 
  Freedom of expression (section 21) • 
  Peaceful assembly and freedom of association (section 22) • 
  Taking part in public life (section 23) • 
  Privacy and reputation (section 25) •  Fair hearing (section 31) (c)  How
  are the human rights limited? -  The
  Lessor/managing agent and/or Council may discriminate against a particular
  group of persons i.e. persons displaying threatening, aggressive or violent
  behaviour as defined by the shopping centre rules (the Rules) and council
  policy entitled “Unreasonable Customer Conduct Policy” (the
  Policy). The Rules/Policy effectively removes a person’s entitlement to
  enjoy their other human rights if they engage in particular behaviours. -  It
  is possible that the application of the Rules/Policy may involve the
  humiliation of a person by classing their behaviour as threatening, aggressive
  or violent. -  The
  Rules/Policy includes the option of requesting a person leave Council’s
  premises or having the person removed from Council’s premises (which
  may include security/police involvement). This would curtail a person’s
  right to move freely within Queensland. -  The
  Rules/Policy allows the termination of contact with a person (e.g.
  terminating a phone call), refusing to provide further assistance, limiting
  how contact is made with Council and requests to leave Council
  premises/removal from Council premises. These actions restrict the right of a
  person to express, seek and receive their information and ideas and also
  their right to seek information from Council. -  Person’s
  excluded from Council premises under the Rules/Policy may be denied the right
  to gather together in order to exchange, give or receive information, to
  express views or to conduct a protest or demonstration. -  Persons
  who are restricted/denied from communicating with Council, or attending
  Council premises, may be denied the right to contribute to the political
  process and public governance. -  If
  actions taken under the Rules/Policy are not undertaken lawfully, it is
  possible that the person may have sustained an attack on their reputation. The Rules/Policy potentially restricts access to the
  administrative review of Council decisions for a person by refusing
  assistance or limiting contact with Council. (d) Is
  there a good reason for limiting the relevant rights? Is the limitation fair
  and reasonable? Yes. The purpose of the limitations is important to
  provide a safe environment for staff to work and customers to attend a public
  library, free from offensive, abusive or threatening behaviour and, is a
  legislative obligation for council and a matter of considerable importance.
  Council and shopping centre policies aim to address threatening, aggressive
  or violent behaviour by customers towards Council staff and other customers. (e)  Conclusion The decision is consistent with human rights. Attachments and Confidential Background Papers 1.
