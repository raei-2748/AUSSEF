# Stage 7 results: who lives in towns that get fully cut off?

## Verdict (pre-registered)

**NOT SUPPORTED.** The median share of residents aged 65+ is **27.7%** in the
43 towns that fires have fully cut off, against **23.1%** in the 418 towns never cut off.
The difference is **4.6 percentage points** (95% CI -0.002 to 7.0; Mann–Whitney p = 0.169).

### In plain words

- **80,061 people** live in the 43 towns where fire has cut every road out at least once.
- Among them are **23,873 people aged 65 or over**, **6,251 people who need help with everyday activities**, and **1,378 homes with no car**. These are the people who would find it hardest to get out.
- Cut-off towns are older at the median (27.7% vs 23.1% aged 65+), but the 95% range (-0.002 to 7.0 points) just reaches zero, so by the rule set in advance this is not a clear difference from other towns.

This is descriptive. It does not show that fires caused these patterns, and it says nothing about lives.

## Caveats

- One fire can cut off several neighbouring towns, so the town-level intervals are optimistic.
- The Census was taken in August 2021, after the 2019–20 fires.
- Single-exit towns (outside the stage-2 sample) are not included, though they may be the most exposed.
- Cut-offs come from fire maps (stage 2), so they are upper bounds with no timing.
- Many exit roads are state roads; council money is only part of the picture.
