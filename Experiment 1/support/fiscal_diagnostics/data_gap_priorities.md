# Data-gap priorities

1. **Forecast-time availability and consistent definitions (gatekeeper).** Record publication/revision dates and exact operating-ratio definitions for each fiscal release, and release dates for exposure records. The current backtest assumes availability. Without this, apparent improvements may use information not knowable at the forecast date.
2. **Longer comparable fiscal history.** Add earlier/later annual observations only where council boundaries/entities and accounting definitions can be reconciled. The original training windows have only one, two and three primary outcome years. More council rows in the same years do not solve this. Reserve new future years from model selection.
3. **Underlying operating revenue and expense components.** Audit numerator/denominator and one-off items for the 20 largest movements, the 20 worst relative errors and a comparable set of well-predicted cases. Warren's stable high ratio and Carrathool's large improvement show different failure mechanisms. The CSV cannot establish their accounting causes. Inspect audited accounts/annual reports before attributing changes to disasters, grants or rebuilding.
4. **Operating grants versus capital/disaster grants, and exact funding/reimbursement timing.** The aggregate `grants_pct` field is not sufficient to reconstruct accounting treatment, receivables or recognition timing. Collect amounts, periods and announced/received/recognised dates separately. Only genuinely forecast-time-known information is eligible as a predictor; realised future receipts are diagnostic outcomes, not predictors.
5. **Physical flood exposure and local infrastructure damage.** Declaration counts are administrative onsets, not intensity. Seek event dates, inundated assets/area and initial damage assessments with coverage and publication dates. No declared onset is not a physical zero. Damage/reconstruction estimates are candidate inputs only when available by the forecast origin; later final costs belong in a retrospective audit.
6. **Routine maintenance versus disaster reconstruction and reconstruction cost.** Separate recurring maintenance requirements/actual spending from restoration and capital works, maintaining a reconciliation to accounts. This is particularly important for the secondary maintenance result, whose ratio can mask different activities. Never recode missing cost as zero.

These priorities follow the observed temporal shortage, missed operating movements and unused coarse exposure measures. They are hypotheses about better measurement, not promises of predictive improvement. Do not add all fields indiscriminately. Establish coverage and timing first, then fix a small specification before evaluating on untouched future data.
