# Experiment 3 — measurement quality and decision

**NO-GO for causal estimation with the evidence recovered in this pilot. GO for targeted ledger collection.** No claim of a zero effect is warranted.

## Scope and selection

This is a purposive feasibility pilot, not an exhaustive search of all NSW councils. Three affected cases were selected before examining their maintenance outcomes: Eurobodalla FY2019–20 (Black Summer, followed by February flooding), Lismore and Richmond Valley FY2021–22 (February/March floods). All belong to the existing 103 continuing councils. Their prominent disasters and accessible official-report archives motivated selection, not whether maintenance fell. Lismore and Richmond Valley share an event group and are not two independent disasters.

The audit screened 306 possible treated–comparison pairs. Four links were retained for document review: Eurobodalla–Griffith, Eurobodalla–Kiama, Lismore–Wagga Wagga, Richmond Valley–Wagga Wagga. The candidate panel contains 36 unique council-years across six councils, covering three pre-years, the event year and two subsequent years. It is **not an estimation-ready panel**. A council used as a candidate twice is not duplicated in the annual panel.

There were 35 source access attempts, including document indexes; 20 downloaded PDFs. Detailed annual reports, financial statements, operational plans and a recovery plan were searched; 37 numerical or qualitative observations were curated. Keyword screening is logged separately from close-reading and transcription. Not every selected year has a directly reviewed original budget. Unsuccessful access and incomplete searches are not evidence that the underlying records do not exist.

## A. Can routine maintenance be separated reliably?

**Partly at individual budget-line level; not sufficiently to construct any certified primary outcome.** The primary denominator must be an adopted plan fixed before the event, with a positive amount. The numerator must cover exactly the same asset, fund, work type and annual accounting scope and exclude disaster response/reconstruction. The primary outcome is `(actual routine − original planned routine) / original planned routine`, expressed as a fraction. Multiply by 100 only for presentation as a percentage. Actual and planned values must not come from different years. No outcome is imputed.

- Lismore's [2022–23 budget, p33](https://www.lismore.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/2/5.-council/7.-about-council/6.-corporate-information/2022-2023-budget-by-program.pdf) labels three different columns: **actual 2020–21**, **original budget 2021–22**, and **draft budget 2022–23**. Urban sealed-road maintenance is $1,297,400, $1,506,600 and $2,450,600 respectively. These are not a same-year actual/budget pair. A distinct disaster-related expense line exists, but its existence alone does not certify that all disaster costs were excluded from other maintenance codes.
- Richmond Valley's [adopted 2021–22 plan, pp18–20](https://richmondvalley.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Operational-Plan-2021-2022-including-Financial-Estimates-2021-2025-Adopted-by-Council-22-June-2021-1.pdf) lists maintenance components separately from named flood works. The plan was adopted on 22 June 2021, confirmed by its cover and audited financial statement B5-1. Examples include Casino urban road maintenance $439,782 and two unsealed grading budgets of $489,673 and $489,678. These are **components**, not complete council routine-maintenance totals. Matching actuals were not recovered. Text extraction supports the amounts; the local PDF renderer could not display this document's fonts reliably, so it is explicitly not marked visually verified.
- The [2021–22 annual report, printed p66](https://richmondvalley.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Annual-Report-2021-2022-FINAL.pdf) reports $50,000 against an incremental road-maintenance SRV item. That restricted funding item cannot stand in for all routine spending or be paired with a differently scoped budget.
- The existing OLG required-maintenance denominator is an asset-management-plan estimate. [Griffith's infrastructure schedule, PDF p7](https://www.griffith.nsw.gov.au/files/sharedassets/public/v/1/cm-documents/2019-2020-special-schedule.pdf) states that basis. It is not the requested adopted budget. All inherited maintenance adequacy ratios remain secondary.

The panel therefore has **0 certified primary deviations**. Candidate component amounts remain in the evidence ledger rather than being promoted to complete totals. Disaster funding, capital expenditure, damage estimates and annual advance receipts are kept separate. No grants are subtracted from maintenance to invent a routine figure.

## B. Are there enough comparable affected and comparison observations?

**No.** Pre-event screening uses maintenance level and three-year trend, cash cover, current ratio, operating position, own-source ratio, reviewed debt coverage, population, road length and prior hazard measures when complete for the affected council. Cash, debt coverage and other variables are not imputed. Common numeric inputs are scaled by the interquartile range across pre-event observations, then absolute differences are averaged. This is a transparent candidate-ranking distance, not an estimated treatment propensity or a fiscal-health index. Population and nominal maintenance are log-transformed for screening only. The source classification is matched exactly, but is not geographic region/remoteness. A geography/remoteness crosswalk has not been verified.

There are 12 possible numeric screen features. Ten are usable for Eurobodalla, eleven for the Northern Rivers cases; the exact list is retained for every pair. Incomplete feature vectors are not silently ranked with fewer features. Distance and ranking use no post-disaster fiscal outcomes. Concurrent declarations or positive mapped fire are used only for a conservative exposure exclusion; this excludes any detected exposure, not a statistically estimated definition of “major”. A recorded absence of declarations is not proof of zero physical exposure. Physical flood coverage remains unknown.

For Eurobodalla, 83/102 potential comparisons have documented concurrent exposure, 15 have a different prior classification and four remain provisional. For each Northern Rivers case, 93/102 have concurrent exposure, eight differ in classification and only Wagga Wagga remains provisional. Only the nearest two survivors per case are reviewed in depth. All selected candidates have a later declaration within the event-to-+2-year window. They therefore cannot serve as uninterrupted untreated controls over the full follow-up; any future design would have to censor/redefine their eligible periods and reassess support.

Three pre-years of **aggregate** maintenance were reconstructed. From the first pre-year to the last, Eurobodalla's index rises from 100 to 171.39, compared with 106.55 for Griffith and 133.57 for Kiama; Richmond Valley reaches 134.50 against Wagga Wagga's 99.99. Lismore reaches 96.79 against 99.99. These are nominal aggregate trajectories; scope changes, asset mix and earlier disasters can explain differences. Similar-looking aggregate paths do not validate unobserved routine-maintenance pre-trends. No significance test or assertion of parallel trends is made. There are only two selected event groups, too few to justify ordinary event-cluster asymptotics.

## C. Is there evidence of routine-maintenance crowd-out?

**Not established.** There is evidence of capital-project disruption and resource reprioritisation, alongside evidence that scheduled maintenance continued:

- Richmond Valley's [audited accounts, B5-1, PDF p30](https://richmondvalley.nsw.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Annual_Financial_Statements-2022.pdf) describe a $17.03m capital-expenditure undershoot in connection with floods, prolonged wet weather and supply/contractor constraints. This is a capital finding, not the proposed routine-maintenance outcome.
- Lismore's [accounts, B5-1, PDF p31](https://www.lismore.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/2/5.-council/7.-about-council/6.-corporate-information/annual_financial_statements-2022.pdf) describe capital projects delayed during emergency work. Materials/services expenditure of $60.171m versus a $51.367m budget is a broad mixed expense category and cannot identify routine spending.
- Eurobodalla's [annual report, printed p119](https://www.esc.nsw.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0011/163001/E.ANNUAL-REPORT-2019-2020-FINAL.pdf) associates some capital delays with resources shifted to bushfire response while reporting scheduled road inspection and maintenance completed. This is informative counterevidence to a universal routine-maintenance decline, but no annual dollar pair was recovered. The official PDF was readable through indexed text; direct PDF access returned 403, so table-image verification was unavailable.

These reports make displacement plausible for some work, but do not identify its routine-maintenance magnitude or causal counterfactual. A delayed capital project can reflect access, wet weather, labour or procurement constraints rather than weak fiscal capacity. Aggregate maintenance changes are never labelled crowd-out.

## D. Is the effect larger for fiscally constrained councils?

**Not estimable.** Pre-specified separate indicators are cash cover below three months and operating performance below zero, using the last full pre-event year and never contemporaneous liquidity/grants. All three affected councils have pre-event cash cover above three months (Eurobodalla 14.07; Lismore 13.33; Richmond Valley 11.31), so this pilot has no low-cash treated contrast. Lismore and Richmond Valley have negative prior operating ratios, but they share the flood event. Outcome failure and event/fiscal overlap preclude interpretation of an interaction. The V2 economic values refer to pre-event periods; point-in-time publication/revision availability is not certified.

## E. Can advance/Tripartite funding be analysed?

**NO-GO.** Lismore's [2023–24 accounts, B5-1, PDF p28](https://www.lismore.nsw.gov.au/files/assets/public/v/2/5.-council/7.-about-council/6.-corporate-information/annual_financial_statements-2024.pdf) confirm $86m of TfNSW road-disaster advances received during that financial year. They do not establish the exact first payment day or each project allocation. The [29 March 2024 announcement](https://www.lismore.nsw.gov.au/Council/Latest-news-and-updates/Latest-news/Lismore-Secures-Historic-Funding-Agreement-for-860-Million-Roads-and-Bridges-Rebuild) describes an $860m reconstruction envelope; it is neither a payment record nor a certified effective date. The [statewide July release](https://www.nsw.gov.au/media-releases/new-agreements-deliver-296-million-of-road-repair-funding-just-three-months) describes more than $296m paid across councils during April–June, not council-level payment histories. Neither amount is substituted for expenditure. No zero is assigned to councils without recovered funding records.

## F. What data would unlock the design?

1. Original adopted budgets and subsequent revisions, with adoption dates and stable work-order/account codes, for at least three pre-event years, the event year and two post-years.
2. Monthly actual expenditure by the same asset class, fund and work code, explicitly separating routine planned/corrective maintenance, emergency works, immediate reconstruction, permanent reconstruction, renewal and new capital. Require labour/plant overhead rules, reclassifications and a reconciliation to audited totals. Start with the specific recovered Lismore and Richmond Valley road codes rather than requesting undifferentiated council totals.
3. A job-level register of deferred tasks, original and revised due dates, value and documented reason. Distinguish financially deferred work from work prevented by weather, access, staffing or procurement.
4. Event-linked damage assessments and reconstruction payments/work costs, with AGRN, asset/project IDs and repair scope; overlapping events and scope changes must be traceable.
5. For funding: signed agreement/effective dates, claim and approval dates, every bank receipt/advance/reimbursement, amount, project/event linkage, selection/eligibility rules and existing advance arrangements.
6. Comparable ledgers for potential controls, verified geographic/remoteness and asset-scope comparability, complete event screening over the window and substantially more independent councils/events. Better pre-trend data are a gate, not a guarantee of causal identification.

No outreach was sent. Public evidence already identifies useful budget codes; this is a targeted administrative-data gap, not a reason to fit more complex models.

## Status conventions and audit limits

`observed` means the source reports a value, not that it is suitable for the primary outcome. `verified_zero` is used only for an explicit numeric zero in a specified budget line; it does not mean zero work or zero disaster. `missing` means the requested usable amount was not recovered. `unknown` means scope, exposure, phase or timing is unresolved. `not_applicable` is used for the monetary value of qualitative evidence. Blank amounts are never recoded as zero. “No listed declaration” and “zero within mapped scope” remain narrower than zero physical disaster exposure.

Amounts in financial statements labelled $000 were multiplied by 1,000; budget figures in dollars were not. No currency deflation, aggregation across unmatched scopes or monetary damage-to-expenditure conversion was applied. PDF page references are one-based; printed page references are supplied where different. Retrieval dates and HTTP Last-Modified are not publication dates. The raw source files and their hashes are in `source_provenance.csv`; V2-derived values retain original workbook/sheet/row references in `backbone_value_provenance.csv`.
