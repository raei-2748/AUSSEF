Webcasting and Emergency Plan Statement

Pre-registered members of the public who address Council at the Addresses to
Council Forum and those                      who attend Council Meetings should be aware that their
image, and comments will be recorded, and broadcast live from Council's website.

Council broadcasts live to enhance the accessibility of Council Meetings to the
broader Shellharbour City Community. Recordings can be downloaded from
Council's website for later viewing, or purchased from Council for viewing on a
computer.

For further information on Privacy please refer to the Public Gallery Conduct
Standards displayed in the Chamber.

Council accepts no responsibility for any defamatory or offensive statements   .
Members of the gallery should also                                    refer to the Public Gallery Conduct Standards,
for appropriate standards of behaviour during Council Meetings.

It should be noted that the Addresses to Council Forum is held prior to the Council
Meeting from 4.30pm to 5.30pm.

Should Council need to consider confidential matters during a Council Meeting,
invited members of the gallery may a             ddress the Council on the reasons why the
meeting should not resol ve into Committee of the W hole (Confidential Session).

Please ensure that mobile phones and other electronic devices are turned off or are
in silent mode for the duration of the meeting.

In the event of an emergency, please evacuate the building using the nearest exit
and follow the instructions given by the wardens. Do not     use the lifts.

Disability Access and Inclusion Plan 2017-                          2021
To contribute to Shellharbour City being a more accessible, age-            friendly and
inclusive community, Council adopted the Disability Access and Inclusion Plan 2017-
2021: An Inclusive City.                    The plan will assist Council to effectively plan for and
deliver on the diverse needs of people with disability in the community. The plan
covers four main focus areas: developing positive                  community attitudes and
behaviours; creating liveable communities; supporting access to meaningful
employment and improving access to services through better systems and
processes.
Council is continually working to improve accessibility in the City for people of all
abilities.  The Disability Access Committee provided input in the review of the
Development Control Plan (DCP) with consideration given to enhancements such as
provisions for motorised scooters, roll top kerb ramps at intersections and access
points, roundabouts and kerb ramps in carparks.  Various access improvement
projects were subsequently completed in the 2016-                            17 year

Food Organics Garden Organics (FOGO)
The introduction of the weekly FOGO bin service on 1 July 2016 has seen an
increase in the amount of organic material collected through the kerbside service
from 8,708 tonnes in the 2015-                         16 year to 11,560 tonnes in 2016-                                                   17             –      a 25%
increase in 12 months.   A total of 1,144 residents have also reduced the size of their
red lid (general waste) bin from 240L to 140L this past year.   During 2016/17 the
amount of waste collected through the red lid kerbside bin has reduced by 2,047.62
tonnes when compared to 2015/16 tonnages resulting in an overall reduction to
landfill.

Shell Cove Project

The Shell Cove Project is the major economic development, tourism infrastructure
and job generation initiative undertaken by Council.     It is a 3,000 lot master-planned
residential and mixed-                               use project all focussed around a boat harbour that directly
accesses the Pacific Ocean. The total economic impact of this project in net present
value terms is $3.71 billion over a 20-                           year period.
Major progress was made in the 2016-                                                                          17 with the following milestones achieved:
     •                       The $23 million        contract to construct the breakwater, groyne, entrance
          channel, and the commencement of construction was awarded to Coastwide
          Civil was achieved a year ahead of scheduled date.
     •                                           The excavation of the boat harbour is finished with the harbour edge visual      ly
          evident by the vertical concrete walls, the sloping rock revetments and the
          timber piles of the boardwalk and main wharf jetty.
     •                                                 Subdivision civil works completed for the first stage of Precinct B1/C1 and the
          road construction completed on the first  major section of Harbour Boulevard
          (off Cove Boulevard) and adjacent to the town centre.

Shellharbour Civic Centre

The Civic Centre construction has seen major progress during 2016/                17.  The project
team has been working closely with the contractor to    monitor the project and update
stakeholders.

By the end of 2016 -17, significant construction was achieved; the wave roof
installation was almost complete; cladding to auditorium was nearing completion;
landscape works commenced including installation of a                                  number of Cabbage Tree
palms to the Civic Square and concrete work commenced in the civic square.
Finalisation of all outstanding design packages was completed. This included
signage, furniture and public art. The finalisation of all outstanding planning
approvals was also achieved.  The building was watertight and fit out is well under
wa y.

Fit for the Future

Council continued to  focus  on achieving the seven ratios identified in the NSW  State
Government’s ‘Fit for the Future’ reforms. Council demonstrated its ability to meet all
of these ratios which resulted in a true partnership between Council staff, the
community and Councillors.

Part of Council’s ‘Fit for the Future’ effort focused on an infrastructure renewal
backlog using resources made availabl  e to Council by the community as part of the
Special Rate Variation (SRV). These funds have directly improved the assets and
amenity of the city.

Merge Proposal

Council has transformed from being subject to a proposal to merge with W ollongong
City Council, to a stand           -alone status.

Since the announcement merger proposal defeat of February, 2017, Council has
swiftly moved to commencing a series reforms, that will see the organisation
implement business improvement programs, continuous improvident strategies,
cultural and structural change and a focus on the Community, Councillors, the
Customer and the Council.

2.        Audited Financial Statements

Council must present its Financial Statements at a meeting of Council and give
public notice of the date of the meeting. A summary of the financial statements must
be included in the notice. Copies of the statements must be available for public
inspection at Council’s Administrative Centre from the date of public notice until one
day after the Council Meeting. Any person may make written submissions to the
Council on the statements.

Public notice has been given and copies of the statements have been made
available for public inspection at Council’s Administrative Centre and are also
available on Council’s website. The closing date for submissions in writing is 4.30pm
on Tuesday 7 November 2017;          seven days after the statements have been
presented to Council as per the Local Government Act. The Act requires any written
submissions made on the Financial Statements to be referred to Council's auditor for
consideration.    At the date of writing this report, no written submissions have been
received.

The annual Audited Financial Statements have been endorsed by the Governance,
Risk and Audit Committee at its meeting on 18 October 2017.

Australian Accounting Standards state that general purpose financial statements
shall provide information useful to users for making and evaluat    ing decisions about
the allocation of scarce resources. The format of these statements is also prescribed
by the Standards. To provide information useful for those purposes, general purpose
financial statements of local governments need to disclose information about the
performance and financial position of those local government entities.

The financial statements for the period ending 30 June 2017 presented to Council for
adoption have been prepared as per Australian Accounting Standards and include
the following information as at the period ending date:

•                                                        Income Statement and associated notes which detail the cost of goods and
       services provided and the extent to which that cost was recovered from
       revenues, during the reporting period. The expenses and re venues are
       reported in both type and activity format.

This Planning Proposal is initiated                                                 by Council staff and not the landowner as it is part

Shellharbour LEP 2013 at its Extra-                  ordinary meeting of May 2012.  Of the 1060ha
deferred at that time, there is currently approximately 642ha of deferred land
remaining in the Local Government Area.  The subject land is approximately 8.69ha.

Background

A Planning Proposal is a document that explains the intended effect of a proposed
amendment to a Local Environmental Plan (LEP), such as a rezoning or a land
reclassification, to amend Shellharbour LEP 2013.  A Planning Proposal sets out the
justification for making the amendment which is known as the Gateway Process.

The Gateway Process has the following five key steps:

1.                                  Planning Proposal -                         Council is responsible for the preparation of a Planning
         Proposal, which explains the effect of and justification for the plan.

2.                                                       Gateway             -      The Minister (or delegate) for the Department of Planning and
         Environment determines whether  the Planning Proposal is to proceed.  This
         gateway acts as a checkpoint to ensure that the proposal is justified before
         further studies are done and resources are allocated to the preparation of a
         plan.  A community consultation process is also determined at this time.
         Consultations occur with relevant public authorities and, if necessary, the
         proposal is varied.

3.                          Community consultation                                                                -       the proposal is publicly exhibited (generally low
         impact proposals for 14 days, others for 28 days).  A person making                    a
         submission may also request a public hearing be held.

4.                                                       Assessment           -      Council will consider public submissions and the proposal is
         varied as necessary prior to adoption.  Parliamentary Counsel then prepares a
         draft local environmental plan —                                                          the legal instrument.

5.                                                       Decision       -    With the Minister’s (or delegate’s) approval the plan becomes law
         and is published on the   NSW  legislation website.

         The final decision on whether the plan becomes law and in what form it
         becomes law, rests with the Minister for Planning.

This Planning Proposal is currently at step 4.

The key purpose of the Planning Proposal is to resolve the deferred matter and bring
the subject land into Shellharbour Local Environmental Plan 2013 (LEP 2013).
Council resolved on 4 July 2017 to revise the initial planning proposal (which
considered a residential zoning) to an alternative zone, SP1 Special Activities
(educational establ ishment and place of public worship), submit to the Department of
Planning and Environment (DP&E) requesting a Gateway determination and place
on public exhibition.

Consideration of a revised planning proposal with an alternative zone was required
due to the land being identified as flood prone to an extent that it would require
significant flood investigations to potentially zone to a residential zoning.  As the key
purpose of the proposal is to resolve the deferred matter, there was no urgency to
use additional resources to pursue further flood investigations by Council or the land
owner at this stage.

The revised Planning Proposal was submitted to the DP&E and an amended
Gateway determination was received on 26 July 2017 which supported the SP1
Zone                                                           and also a Floor Space Ratio of 0.5:1 and subsequently the revised Planning
Proposal was placed on public exhibition between 23 August 2017 and 29
September 2017.

                          (10954029)

Summary
The purpose of this report is to provide recommendations to endorse the updated
Terms of Reference for the Lake Illawarra Estuary Management Committee to
include two Aboriginal community representatives and update references to
legislation; and endorse the extension of the current membership for 12 months.
The preparation of the Coastal Management Program (CMP) is underway, with the
current Lake Illawarra E stuary Management Committee committed to the agreed
process and scope of the consultancy. A Project Management Team has been
formed to deliver the CMP under the guidance of the Committee.

The endorsement of the updated Terms of Reference (   Attachment 1) and extension
of the current membership for the next 12 months (  Attachment 2) will allow the
committee to provide continuity in its guidance in developing the CMP for Lake
Illawarra.

Background
In July 2014, the NSW Government disbanded the Lake Illawarra A   uthority, and
handed back the responsibility for managing the Lake to Shellharbour and
Wollongong City Councils, and to a number of state agencies.  Shellharbour and
Wollongong City Councils subsequently endorsed the formation of the Lake Illawarra
Estuary  Management Committee, as part of the joint management arrangements for
the Lake.

The Lake Illawarra Estuary Management Committee Terms of Reference (ToR) was
finalised on 16 December 2014 and a Memorandum of Understanding relating to the
joint working arrangements between the two councils was authorised on 26 April
2015.  There are no amendments proposed to the Memorandum of Understanding
(Attachment 3).

The recruitment of Committee members proceeded in accordance with the ToR, as
well as Shellharbour and W ollongong City Council’s relevant policy and procedures.
The first meeting took place on 12 August 2015 and the Committee has met a total
of nine times.

The Committee was reviewed in accordance with the ToR in mid-                      2017, with results
indicating that it was working well with regards to composition, relationships,
compliance and impact.  All survey respondents wanted to remain active
participants.  Some concerns were raised with regards to the relevant lack of
Aboriginal representation on the Committee.  T  his was consistent with concerns
raised by members of the Shellharbour City Council Aboriginal Advisory Committee.
These concerns have been addressed through the proposed changes to the ToR
(Attachment 1).

The updated ToR                                      states that two Aboriginal community members will be members of
the Committee, one being nominated from the Shellharbour City Council Aboriginal
Advisory Committee and one being nominated from the Wollongong City Council
Aboriginal Reference Group.  This   replaces the current arrangements which was that
one Aboriginal community member was chosen after agreement was reached
between relevant staff at both councils.  The current Aboriginal community member
is a current member of the W ollongong City Council Aboriginal Reference Group so
will have an opportunity to nominate for one of the positions.  Other changes are
recommended to update agency names and terminology where appropriate.

Council has developed the Shellharbour Coastal Zone Management Plan (CZMP)
under the Coastal Protection Act                                                  1979 for our open coastline (beaches).  This plan
does not include Lake Illawarra.  The Lake Illawarra Estuary Management
Committee originally undertook to develop a CZMP for the Lake, however a recent
change in legislation from the Coastal Protection Act                                                                                        (1979) to the Coastal
Management Act        (2016) has facilitated a name change for new management
documents.  All management documents prepared for the coastline (both open coast
and estuaries) will now be titled a Coastal Management Program (CMP).  The Lake
Illawarra CMP will reference the Shellharbour CZMP however they remain separate
documents.

The development of the CMP                                                                       for Lake Illawarra is progressing in accordance with
agreed milestones.  It is expected that the draft CMP                      will be ready for circulation to
the Committee and stakeholders at the end of 2017.  A period of public exhibition will

Background
What is a planning proposal?

A planning proposal is a document that explains the intended effect of a proposed
amendment to a Local Environmental Plan (LEP), such as a rezoning or a land
reclassification, to amend Shellharbour LEP 2013.  A planning proposal sets out the
justification for making the amendment which is known as the gateway process.

The gateway process has the following key steps:

1.                                Planning proposal   -      Council is responsible for the preparation of a planning
          proposal, which explains the effect of and justifi   cation for the plan.

2.                                                       Gateway             -      The Minister (or delegate) for the Department of Planning and
          Environment determines whether the planning proposal is to proceed.  This
          gateway acts as a checkpoint to ensure that the proposal is justified before any
          further studies are done and resources are allocated to the preparation of a
          plan.  A community consultation process is also determined at this time.
          Consultations occur with relevant public authorities and, if necessary, the

Function centres currently prohibited

Function centres are currently prohibited under                            Shellharbour LEP 2013                                                                          in the RE1
Public Recreation zone. The proposed                            additional permitted use and reclassification
would enable the land to be lawfully used for this purpose and will enable                                                                   Council to
enter into a  long term lease of the buildings.

Function centres are defined under LEP 2013 as:

function centre                         means a building or place used for the holding of events, functions,
conferences and the like, and includes convention centres, exhibition centres and
reception centres, but does not include an entertainment facility.

Some types of functions can fall under LEP 2013’s definition of community facility
and are therefore permissible in the RE1 Public Recreation zone. Noting the
community facility definition in italic below, these are functions such as conferences,
sports dinners and social club meetings for the physical, social, cultural or intellectual
development of the community.

 community facility means a building or place:
(a)owned or controlled by a public authority or non-                                                                                                                            profit community organisation,
and
(b)  used for the physical, social, cultural or intellectual development or welfare of the
community,
but does not include an educational establishment, hospital, retail premises, place of
public worship or residential accommodation.

However, it is unlikely  that functions in the nature of either weddings or other private
parties would constitute a use of the building for the physical, social, cultural or
intellectual development or welfare of the community. These types of functions would
fall under the LEP 2013 definition of function centre.


Effect of the planning proposal submission

The planning proposal submission seeks to permit a full array of function types, not
just those types that can fall under the definition of community facility. It does this by
specifying function centres as an additional permitted use on the subject RE1 Pu     blic
Recreation zoned land, but not on any other RE1 land.

11.3.4                                       Renewal of Alcohol-Free Zones and Prohibited Zones
                           (10954818)


To the General Manager
Directorate:                                                Community and Customers
Department:                                    Community Connections

Manager:                                                                                                                           Kathr yn Baget-Juleff - Group Manager Community Connections

Summary
The purpose of this report is to seek       Council's resolution to exhibit the propose  d
renewal     of the 17                                                                       Alcohol-Free and Prohibited Zones listed in  Attachment 1                                 for a
further 4 years.

Re-establishment    of Alcohol-Free and Prohibited Zones at these sites is consistent
with NSW  Local Government Act 1993, and will seek to discourage alcohol                                             -related
crime and                          anti-social behaviour in public places.   The purpose of this renewal is t  o re-
establish the zones that are approaching their expiry date, and to bring the dates for
all of the other zones in line.

Background
The public consumption of alcohol and the associated intoxication and antisocial
behaviour can be intimidating to the general public. This can lead to criminal
offences being committed, such as malicious damage, assault and offensive
conduct. The prohibition of the public consumption of alcohol can assist in
addressing such problems where they have been deemed to be an issue.

The Alcohol-Free and Prohibited Zones listed in this report have been established
over time in response to issues relating to the public consumption of alcohol and the
NSW  Police can use these zones as a tool to reduce alcohol related crime in these
high-risk areas. The Alcohol -Free Zones have reassured the safety and well   -being of
the local population, assisted in deterring public consumption of alcohol, helped
reduce the incidence of antisocial behaviour and have been essential for Police to be
able to regulate the area and enforce prohibition.

Many of these zones are approaching their expiration dates on 17 December 2017,
which presents a good opportunity for council to re-                                          establish these zones and bring
them in line. The benefits of continuing these zones is considered important to
overall community wellbeing.

The renewal of these 17 zones has been supported by the Commander of th    e Lake
Illawarra Local Area Command. The letter of their support can be found in
Attachment 2.

to be updated.

Legal and Policy implications

Under the ‘Ministerial Guidelines for Alcohol Free Zones 2009’                        , an Alcohol-Free
Zone is referred to footpaths, car parks and roads. A Prohibited Zone refers to public
land and reserves.     Under the Local Government Act 1993, a Council may declare
any public road, car park or footpath                         to be an Alcohol -Free Zone. Council may al                                                 so
declare public spaces to be   Alcohol Prohibited Zones . Council is empowered under
the Local Government Act 1993 to establish or re-                                                    establish                                         Alcohol-Free and
Prohibited Zones for periods up to four years.

The Ministerial Guidelines state Council must follow the same process to re-                                                     establish
a zone as it uses to establish a new zone. The guidelines also state that certain
Councils must consult with the Anti  -Discrimination board when establishing an
Alcohol Free Zone, however Shellharbour City Council are n ot listed for this
requirement.

Public / Social Impact

NSW  Police support the renewal of the zones to prohibit the consumpt    ion of alcohol
in these locations. The zones are beneficial to the safety of the public and failure to
renew them could result in i ntoxication issues rising in the areas.    The renewal of the
zones will reduce anti -social behaviour associated with alcohol and intoxication,
which can be intimidating to the general public.

Summary
The purpose of this report is to provide information to Council as per the following
resolution of Council at its meeting of 15 August 2017:

           'That given recent revelations about the recycling industry in Australia, that
           Council officers provide a report on the final destination of recycled material
           collected in Shellharbour City Council recycling bins. The report shall also
           include information on how Counc il can help support the Australian recycling
           industry through its procurement process  .'

Background
Councils kerbside recycling is collected by Remondis and transported to VISY’s
Transfer Station at Kembla Grange. From here, the material is loaded into transport
vehicles and transported to VISY’s sorting facility at Smithfield.

Once the material is sorted, each product type is sent to the following destination:

•                                                                                Paper and cardboard are utilised at VISY’s paper mills to make new paper and
           cardboard products.
•                                      PET plastics are utilised in VISY’s 100% recycled plastics plant. These
           products are recycled into new PET products.
•                                                                                           Steel is traded locally and internationally.
•                                                                                  Glass is sent to Glass Recovery Services (GRS) in Penrith. Material processed
           through GRS is utilised as a sand replacement in construction works, filtration
           media for pool, industrial and environmental filtration, sand blast medium and
           rendering and non-                                                   slip applications.
•                                                                                           Aluminium is trading locally and internationally.
•                                           HDPE plastics are utilised in VISY’s 100% recycled plastics plant. These
           products are recycled into new HDPE products.
•                                                                                           Mixed plastics are trading locally and internationally.
•                                                                                           Contamination (waste) is disposed of a W hytes Gully landfill at Kembla Grange.
