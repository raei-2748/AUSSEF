Introduction

Forbes Shire Council is proud to present its Annual Report for 2023/24. The Annual Report is one of the key
points of accountability between Council and its community. This Annual Report focuses on four sections:

    i.   a summary of the activities Council has carried out through its annual Operational Plan - this
         shows how Council has performed against the tasks it set itself in the four-year Delivery Program.

    ii.  statutory information - information that it is important for community members to know about and to
         help understand how Council has performed both as a business entity and a community leader.

    iii. financial statements - a copy of the audited financial statements for the year ending 30 June 2024,
         showing Council’s financial position (under separate cover); and

    iv.  other statutory annual reporting - public sector agencies are required to report annually under the
         Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009, Public Interest Disclosure Act 1994, Disability
         Inclusion Act 2014 and the Modern Slavery Act 2018.

Legislative Framework
Each of the three spheres of Government in Australia (Federal, State and Local) has different roles and
responsibilities in funding and delivering services to its communities.  There are some services that all local
councils must provide, and some that councils can choose to provide. Local governments in NSW derive their
power  and  responsibilities  from  the  Local  Government  Act  1993 (NSW)  which  gives  councils  a  charter
comprising principles which drive councils’ functions. These include community leadership; custodianship and
trusteeship of public assets; facilitating development, improvement and coordination of local communities; and
an obligation to inform their communities and the State Government of the council’s activities.
In addition to the Local Government Act there are more than 135 other pieces of legislation which influence the
work of councils in NSW.
Intended Audience
This report provides information to a broad range of stakeholders including Forbes Local Government Area
(LGA) residents and ratepayers, government and non-government organisations, business partners, and local
businesses. It also provides Council’s workforce with information on how well it has performed over the year and
how their efforts are contributing to achieve our community’s vision.

A message from the Mayor and General Manager

We are proud to present the Annual Report for 2023/24, a year marked by both achievements and challenges
as we continue to serve the needs and aspirations of our community. This report reflects Council’s
commitment to transparency, good governance, and accountability, as well as its dedication to delivering the
vision outlined in the Forbes Community Strategic Plan (CSP).
This past year has seen the successful completion of several key projects that have made a real difference in
people’s lives. Whether it's enhancing local infrastructure, improving community services, or progressing
Council’s sustainability agenda, each achievement reflects Council’s strategic focus on building a vibrant and
resilient future for Forbes. Some of Council’s proudest accomplishments include the transformation of Pitt Stop
Park, construction of the Multipurpose Equestrian Centre arena and installation of walking paths from Nelson
Park to Edward Street, connecting the CBD with Goldridge Estate.
Of course, this year has not been without its challenges. Like many communities across New South Wales,
Council has faced ongoing pressure from external factors such as the delayed payment of disaster funding
from other levels of government and significant flood repair work from the 2022 flood, and competition with the
public and private sectors for staff, contractors and materials. Despite these obstacles, the resilience of our
staff and the determination of Council has enabled us to stay on course, ensuring that essential services
continued to be delivered efficiently, and key projects remained on track.
As we reflect on the achievements of the past year, we wish to acknowledge the hard work and dedication of
our staff, as well as the support and engagement of our community. Without this collaboration, our successes
would not have been possible. We also want to extend our gratitude to our councillors, whose leadership and
commitment to our shared vision have been invaluable.
Together, we look forward to building on the foundation we have laid, creating a future that reflects the values,
needs, and aspirations of our community.
Thank you for your continued trust and partnership as we continue to make Forbes a better place for all.

Your Council
Forbes Shire Council administers a Local Government Area covering 4,717km², which includes the urban area
of Forbes plus the village areas of Bedgerabong, Wirrinya, Corinella, Garema, Calarie and Ootha. Forbes LGA
is bordered by Lachlan, Parkes, Cabonne, Weddin and Bland local government areas.
Forbes Shire Council has an annual budget of just under $100M, total assets of approximately $638M and
employs approximately 180 staff. The governing body includes the Mayor (elected by Council) plus eight
Councillors elected for a four-year term.
Councillors
Forbes Shire Council is governed by the body of nine Councillors who are elected by the residents and
ratepayers of the Forbes LGA. The roles of the Councillors, as members of the body corporate, are to direct and
control the affairs of Council in accordance with the Local Government Act 1993 and other applicable legislation.
Leadership Team
The General Manager is responsible for the efficient operation of Council as an organisation and for ensuring
the implementation of Council’s decisions.
The senior leadership team comprises the:
    1.  General Manager
    2.  Director Corporate Services
    3.  Director Engineering
    4.  Director Planning and Growth, and
    5.  Director Projects.
This team is responsible for leadership of staff, management of individual departments, implementation of
strategic initiatives and long-term planning.
The senior leadership team is supported by 14 Managers, who are accountable for day-to-day operations and
ensuring Council’s programs and services are implemented.

Council Decision Making
Council Meetings
In 2023/24 Council held 11 Ordinary Meetings and one Special (Extraordinary) Meeting to which 6 members of
the public addressed Council on various issues and items of business at these meetings.
As well as attending Council meetings, Councillors took part in external and internal committees. These are
another opportunity for Councillors to contribute to issues important to the community.
Section 355 Committees
Council’s Section 355 Committees comprise Councillors and members of the community who provide advisory
input into Council’s activities:

AERODROME: The Committee’s purpose and function is to advise Council on strategic matters relating to the Forbes Aerodrome as
relevant to Council IP&R actions defined in the Community Strategic Plan.
AUDIT, RISK AND IMPROVEMENT: The Committee’s purpose is to review the following aspects of Council’s operations: (a) compliance,
(b) risk management, (c) fraud control, (d) financial management, (e) governance, (f) implementation of the Community Strategic Plan,
Delivery Program and strategies, (g) service reviews, (h) collection of performance measurement data by Council and (i) any other matters
prescribed by the regulations. (Extract from s.428A(2) Audit, Risk and Improvement Committee,                         Local Government Amendment
(Governance and Planning) Act 2016).
ECONOMIC, FINANCE AND INFRASTRUCTURE: The Committee’s purpose is to consider matters related to economic development,
finance, infrastructure as relevant to Council Integrated Planning and Reporting actions defined in the Community Strategic Plan
EVENTS AND TOURISM: The main purpose and function of this Committee is to advise Council on strategic matters relating to events
and tourism from the economic, planning, services, infrastructure, and community engagement perspectives.
FLOODPLAIN: The Committee’s purpose and function is to review and advise Council on floodplain matters relating to development
applications.
FORBES YOUTH ACTION TEAM: The Committee’s purpose and function is to advise Council on strategic opportunities and challenges
impacting the youth demographic in the Forbes LGA.
HERITAGE ADVISORY: The Committee’s purpose and function is to advise Council on strategic matters relating to heritage from the
planning, infrastructure and community engagement prospective.
INDOOR POOL ADVISORY: The purpose of this Committee is to advise Council on strategic and operational matters relating to the
management and operation of the Forbes Indoor Pool.
OLYMPIC POOL ADVISORY COMMITTEE: The purpose of this Committee is to advise Council on strategic and operational matters
relating to the operation of the Forbes Olympic Pool.
SALEYARDS ADVISORY: The Committee’s purpose and function is to advise Council on strategic matters relating to the Central West
Livestock Exchange.
TREE: The Committee’s purpose and function is to advise Council on strategic matters relating to the management of trees in urban and
parkland settings in the Forbes Local Government Area.

16.     People and Strategy Activities undertaken by Council during 2023/24

Council’s People and Strategy division works in partnership with all departments and leaders in the organisation
to ensure the achievement of the agreed objectives through the development of a sustainable and engaged
workforce and an industry aligned cyber security strategy and workplace IT systems.

Employee Performance

Council conducts employee reviews through our Human Resource Management system - ELMO twice annually.
These reviews provide all employees the opportunity to be acknowledged for their skills, competence and
performance through effective, formal, documented feedback as well as the setting of Key Performance
Indicators (KPIs). Employees are given a voice to design their learning plans and reflect of achievements in a
highly structured and consistent way. Supervisors and Managers are provided with ongoing coaching and
mentoring to drive the quality of feedback and performance management positively at Council.

Learning and Development

Council has a strong Learning and Development strategy in place allowing all employees to access high quality
training development activities to drive performance in their roles, and across the business. Council invests over
$250,000 annually to ensure the workforce is safe, compliant and constantly developing. Council currently hosts
11.5% of our employees as trainees and graduates in over 8 different streams. Council has reached an 80%
target of employee pipeline completions, and 85% of these employees go on to secure a full-time role with
Council. This includes trainees, apprentices, graduates, cadets and local high school work placement students
– Council consistently and genuinely demonstrates a strong commitment to ‘growing our own’ and promoting
Council as an employer of first choice in the community.

Council  has  partnered  with  Charles  Sturt  University  and  University  of  Southern  Queensland  to  provide
placements and further education in the Engineering and Building Certification areas to support and encourage
graduates through hosted placement and on the job experience. Council invests over $200,000 every year in
compliance and capability training, leadership development and tertiary training.

Council also offers up to 30 secondment opportunities to staff in a yearly cycle – allowing them to act up into
roles and try new areas of the business out. This has proven very successful in engaging staff, providing diversity
of work and contribution to Council’s ongoing success.

Further Education

Council assisted a number of employees participating in further education including Certificate III and IV,
Graduate Diplomas, Bachelor and Masters level degrees within their chosen career fields, including the areas
of:

         Horticulture Certificates

17.     Work, Health and Safety

Leading a safe workplace has continued to be a key priority for Council during 2023/24 and Council continues
to operate in accordance with the Work Health and Safety Act 2011. Council is continuously improving
Policies, Systems and Processes, in close collaboration with the workforce and is regularly aligning WHS
strategy with the ISO 45001 and Statecover Annual Audits. Council has consistently received between 80+%
compliance in these Audits.

Council inducts 100% of new staff, contractors ad consultants, ensuring all workers under the PCBU of
Council are aware, compliant and engaged in the importance of WHS.  Key focus areas for the 2023/24 FY
were: Contractor Management Framework, establishment of a WHS and Risk Steering Committee for
leadership influence any buy-in, significant increase in onsite Audits and business reporting in the area of
WHS. Incident reporting and management continues to be a key strength in the operation, and building a
strong safety culture from the ground up is continuing to improve over time. WHS now has an active seat at
the procurement and contracting table, ensuring all contractors and parties engaged with Council are
compliant and aware of internal processes and expectations.

27.      Government Information (Public Access) Act and Regulations

s.125 (1) of the Government Information (Public Access) Act 2009 (GIPA Act) and Schedule 2 of the Government
Information (Public Access) Regulation 2018.

Government Information (Public Access) Annual Report

The GIPA Act provides four ways for government information to be released:
i).        Open access information
         All Local Government Authorities must provide open access to information prescribed in Schedule 2 of
         the Regulation. This information includes policies, planning documents, meeting agendas and minutes,
         contracts and development applications.  The majority of this information is available via Council’s
         website
ii).       Proactive release
         Forbes Shire Council has, over several years, had a proactive approach to the release of information
         with Council’s website promoted as the primary source of information for the community. Council also
         proactively provides information to the community via a number of other avenues including Council
         notices published weekly in the Forbes Advocate and Phoenix, media releases, weekly local radio
         segment, social media postings, monthly Council meeting summary, quarterly community newsletters
         and emails to special interest contact groups.
iii).       Informal release of information
         Members of the public may contact Council and ask for information that is not otherwise available. This
         is known as an informal request.
iv).       Formal access application for release of information
         If information cannot be accessed through any of the above ways, members of the public may submit a
         formal access application to Council.

Review of Release of Government Information under s.7(3) Government Information (Public
Access) Act 2009

Council has reviewed the type of information held by Council that should be made publicly available and is
satisfied that all such information is currently available to the public, within the limits of Council’s resources.

Statistical Information about Formal Access Applications
